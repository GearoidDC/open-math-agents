A necessary condition on the prime factors of a five-row value

Let H(a) count the rows n with C(n,k)=a for some 1<=k<=n/2. If
a>=10^100 and H(a)>=5, then a has at least fifteen distinct prime divisors.
This also holds globally using the separate finite exclusion through
10^100 in Erdos849-googol-note.txt. Here is the argument above the cutoff.

Write W for an assumed upper bound on the number of prime divisors, X=10^K,
and y=a/X>=1. For each p|a put Q_p=p^v_p(a). In Legendre's formula for
v_p(C(n,k)) each power p^i contributes either zero or one, and those above
n contribute zero. Consequently Q_p<=n and a<=n^W. Put N=floor(X^(1/W)).
Choose t>W such that C(N,t)>N^W and 2t<=N. The ratio C(n,t)/n^W is
increasing for n>=N, by its product formula with t-W powers of n and t
nondecreasing factors 1-i/n. Hence every admissible column is below t.

The admissible columns contributing different rows are distinct by strict
monotonicity in the row index for each fixed column. At most one row comes
from column 1. Thus H(a)>=5 requires four distinct columns from 2 to t-1.
Theorem 2.2 of Blokhuis, Brouwer and de Weger excludes the nine pairs
(2,3),(2,4),(2,5),(2,6),(2,8),(3,4),(3,6),(4,6),(4,8) in this range.
https://arxiv.org/pdf/1707.06893

For any two other columns j<d, suppose C(n,d)=C(m,j)=a. Assign each Q_p
to a factor n-s, 0<=s<d, where the p-adic valuation is largest. For each
p^i dividing this factor, at most floor((d-1)/p^i) other numerator factors
are divisible by p^i: the total count in the interval is at most
floor((d-1)/p^i)+1. For larger i no other factor is divisible by p^i.
The valuation of the product of the other factors is at most v_p((d-1)!).
Since a is obtained by dividing the numerator by d!, Q_p divides the
selected factor. Apply the same observation to the j-column.

Define b_s=(n-s)/(product of the powers assigned there). Then all b_s
are positive integers and their product equals d!. If e factors are empty
and S' contain exactly one prime power, W>=S'+2(d-e-S'), so at least
S=max(0,2(d-e)-W) of the factors are singletons. Each empty factor costs
at least L=floor((d!X)^(1/d))-d+1 in the product of b_s.

Choose any S singleton factors. Each assigned Q_p divides some m-r in
the j-column, and these powers are pairwise coprime. If h of them divide
one such factor, their product is at most m, and the corresponding product
of their b_s is at least max(1,(n-d+1)^h/m). The logarithm of this
quantity is convex in h, so among all splits of S into j bins the product
of these lower bounds is minimized when occupancies differ by at most one.
Indeed moving one item from a bin of size h to a bin of size <=h-2
cannot increase the product. Take q,r with S=qj+r; use j-r bins of size
q and r of size q+1.

Put R_k=floor((k!X)^(1/k)). If C(u,k)=a and 0<=h<k, the falling product
gives (R_k-h)y^(1/k)<=u-h<=(R_k+k-h)y^(1/k). Thus for a balanced bin of
size h with hj>=d the lower bound is at least max(1,L^h/(R_j+j)),
uniformly for y>=1. A bin with hj<d contributes at least one. Multiply
over the balanced distribution; call the resulting rational bound G.
The pair is impossible at this e if L^e G>d!.

A second bound uses a singleton n-s=b_s Q_p. Choose r with Q_p|(m-r).
In each falling product reduce all factors except that host modulo Q_p.
Cross multiplication now shows that Q_p^2 divides

  (-1)^(d-1-s) A(n-s) - (-1)^(j-1-r) B(m-r),
  A=j!s!(d-1-s)!,   B=d!r!(j-1-r)!.

Set D=A(R_d+d-s), E=B(R_j+j-r). When the signs agree and B(R_j-r)<=D,
assign this r the trivial bound b_s>=1. Otherwise the expression is
nonzero, by the row estimates with 1/j>1/d. Divide by the p-free part
g of gcd(A,B). The resulting positive absolute value is a multiple of
Q_p^2 and at most T y^(1/j), with T=max(D,E)/g for agreeing signs or
T=(D+E)/g for opposite signs. For d<2j this gives

  b_s^2 T >= (R_d-s)^2 y^(2/d-1/j) >= (R_d-s)^2.

Since b_s divides d!, one may round up to the next divisor meeting this
bound (or use d!+1 when none meets it). Minimize over r for each offset
s and each possible base p. All p>d have the same g=gcd(A,B). Dynamic
programming minimizes the product of these bounds over any S distinct
offsets. Each small base p<=d can be used at most once, with no restriction
on the number of distinct larger bases in their common class. Write V for
the minimum, using V=1 if d>=2j. Necessarily L^e V<=d!.

Erdos849GroupedHosts.py checks both inequalities for every feasible e.
For W=13,K=100 it gives t=15 and excludes 45 pairs, meeting all four-column
subsets. This proves the fourteen-prime lower bound directly in that range.
For W=14,K=123 it excludes all four-column subsets unconditionally. For
W=14,K=100 ten subsets survive. Each contains at least one of (7,11),
(10,11),(12,13),(14,15); repeating the product calculation with all
singleton bases larger than d excludes each of those four pairs.

It remains to test n=s+b p^v with 0<=s<d, p<=d prime, v>=1, b|d!, and
10^100<=C(n,d)<=10^123 for those pairs. The program enumerates a superset
of these rows, screens them by 19 primes greater than d, then tests exact
membership in column j. For a surviving value let R=floor((j!a)^(1/j)).
Any solution has R<=m<=R+j, so testing all j+1 rows suffices. The result:

 14  15             151320                   2             0
