Excluding eighteen prime factors when the largest column is at most seventeen

Put X=10^128 and Y=a/X. If a>=X occurs in five admissible rows with
largest chosen column at most 17, then a has at least 19 distinct prime
divisors. The inputs are the published incompatibility of any two
columns in {2,3,4,6} above X and the existing exact interpolation
polynomials. Erdos849NineteenLow.py verifies the following finite steps.

1. An individual bound on every cofactor

Suppose C(n,k)=a. For each prime p dividing a, assign its full power
p^e||a to a term of maximal p-valuation among n,n-1,...,n-k+1.
Let V be that maximal valuation. Write m=floor(log_p k). In the
factorial floor formula for e, at each level i the count of multiples
of p^i amongst the k terms minus floor(k/p^i) is zero or one.
For m<i<=V that difference is always one. Also e<=V, since at levels
i>V the count vanishes. Thus V-e<=m. In particular the full power
divides the chosen term. For p not dividing a, take e=0; the same
count shows V<=m. Any other term has valuation at most m since two
terms divisible by p^(m+1)>k cannot occur. Consequently the integers

  b_s=(n-s)/product of the full powers assigned to slot s

all divide M_k=lcm(1,...,k). Their product is k!, by (n)_k=k!a.

2. Simultaneous use of a column pair

Four different proper columns are necessary for five distinct rows.
At most one can belong to the classified clique {2,3,4,6}. Hence the
two largest satisfy 7<=j<d<=17. For that pair the exact verifier
Erdos849SingletonInterpolation.branch_bounds gives

  P <= Q Y^(1/17)          for ordinary prime bases,
  P <= Q_p Y^(1/17)        for p=2,3,5,7,11,13.

Here Q and Q_p are the maxima of the corresponding local bounds over
both row offsets. If P>d, its multiple in the d consecutive terms is
unique, so the maximal-valuation assignment of section 1 has the
required offsets. Smaller powers satisfy these bounds directly. Put
rho_p=max(1,Q_p/Q) and E=product rho_p, ordering the rho_p in
nonincreasing order.

Test any additional column k, including j and d themselves. Its terms
are at least L Y^(1/k) for L=floor((k!X)^(1/k))-k+1. If a slot has t
assigned powers, put

  C_t=max(1,L/Q^t) for tk<=17, and C_t=1 otherwise.

The slot's cofactor is at least C_t divided by the product of its
exceptional-base loss factors. This follows by comparing the two
estimates for the numerator term and using Y>=1. Thus a slot is
impossible if C_t>M_k times the product of the t largest rho_p
(use all available factors if t exceeds their number). Over the
whole row, product C_t > k! E also gives a contradiction, since
each exceptional prime base is assigned just once.

Under a cap of eighteen primes, pad the allocation by units if necessary
to make eighteen assigned factors. Each unit satisfies the ordinary
bound without any exceptional-base cost and leaves the cofactor
unchanged. The checker enumerates all unordered k-tuples of nonnegative
occupancies summing to eighteen. Reject a column if every tuple fails
one of the preceding comparisons. All comparisons use exact integers
or rational numbers.

Of the 55 possible pairs, all except (9,17) reject j or d in its own
right. With (9,17) present, the only allowable smaller proper columns
are 2,3,4,6. At most one of them can occur, giving no fourth proper
column. Run python Erdos849NineteenLow.py to reproduce the computation.
