Distribution of prime powers among all offset tuples

Fix distinct proper Pascal columns k_1,...,k_r. Put f_k(X)=(X)_k/k!,
D=product k_i, and S=sum_i (k_i-1)/k_i. For an admissible shared
value a>=2, write f_ki(n_i)=a, n_i>=2k_i. An offset tuple u chooses
0<=u_i<k_i for every i. There are exactly D tuples. Define

             H_u=gcd(a,n_1-u_1,...,n_r-u_r).

1. An effective estimate independent of the total number of primes

For every positive integer m there are effectively computable positive
constants c_m,C_m depending only on the columns and m, such that

       c_m a^(1/D - S/m) <= H_u <= C_m a^(1/D + S/m).              (1)

In particular each normalized log H_u is within epsilon of (log a)/D
in the following sense: |log H_u/log a - 1/D|<epsilon above an
effectively computable threshold for each fixed epsilon>0. This proof
works even if some column indices have a common factor and if some
affine points away from t=0 are singular. It uses elementary linear
algebra instead of a genus calculation or prime support restriction.

Consequently, given any fixed selection of offsets in a subset of
columns, each sufficiently large solution has at least product k_j,
over all omitted columns j, different primes common to a and every
selected row factor. This is an effective height restriction on the
specified factor or simultaneous factors. For (5,6,7,8) it requires
at least 336,280,240,210 different primes in each *individual* factor
of columns 5,6,7,8, respectively. For (3,5,7,8) the corresponding
numbers are 280,168,120,105. The threshold for these limiting counts
has not been explicitly evaluated. Section 2 gives smaller all-height
counts with evaluated constants for these same patterns.

Proof of (1). Let R=Q[t,X_1,...,X_r]/(f_ki(X_i)-t). It is free of
rank D over Q[t], with basis product X_i^e_i (0<=e_i<k_i), by
successive reduction by the monic equations (X_i)_ki-k_i! t.
The full-product monodromy argument in Erdos849ReciprocalRunge.txt
proves R is a domain for distinct integer indices. This is the only
geometric irreducibility input here. Each rational point P_u=(0,u)
is smooth and unramified above t=0 because f'_ki(u_i) is nonzero.
Thus its Taylor expansions in the local parameter t are rational and
can be computed recursively from the defining equations.

Select a set of J offset tuples. Put h=floor(Jm/D) and take the span
of all products t^v product X_i^e_i for 0<=v<=h and 0<=e_i<k_i.
Its dimension D(h+1)>Jm. Requiring a zero of order >=m at each of
the J points gives at most Jm rational linear constraints, so there
is a nonzero F in this span with all those zeros. On an integer
solution, n_i<=k_i-1+(k_i!a)^(1/k_i), and hence

                   |F(a,n_1,...,n_r)| <= A_F a^(h+S).

The coefficient norm gives A_F explicitly. At every selected tuple
the function F belongs to the m-th power of its maximal ideal in R:
this holds in the smooth local discrete valuation ring by the jet
test, and the global maximal-ideal power contracts back unchanged,
since its quotient is local Artinian. Express F as a sum of polynomial
multiples of products of m generators among t and X_i-u_i, and clear
rational denominators. With one positive integer c working for the
whole selected set, H_u^m divides cF(a,n_1,...,n_r) for each member.
The coefficients and c can be found by solving linear equations or
by an ideal membership calculation.

For each p^e exactly dividing a there is at least one tuple w with
p^e|H_w. For each row, remove the term of greatest p-valuation. For
every level h>=1 the remaining count of multiples of p^h is at most
floor(k_i/p^h). Thus the removed factor has valuation at least
v_p(k_i!a)-v_p(k_i!)=e. Its offsets across the rows furnish w.

Select just u for the upper estimate in (1). For the lower estimate,
select all D-1 other tuples. If v_p(H_u)<e, any tuple w hosting p^e
is different from u and therefore p^(em) divides the selected cF.
This gives (a/H_u)^m | cF. Combining with h<=Jm/D gives the two
exponents as claimed, off the zero set of F.

The determinant of multiplication by F on the D-element basis over
Q[t] is a nonzero polynomial since R is a domain. If F vanishes at
a solution, the image of multiplication in the specialized algebra
cannot contain one, so the determinant vanishes at t=a. A polynomial
root bound therefore bounds all exceptional integral a effectively.
Taking maxima of A_F,c and finitely adjusting for those small a and
the D choices of u gives the stated uniform constants.

For the prime count choose m>DS. When a exceeds an effective bound,
all H_u exceed lcm(1,...,max k_i-1). Each then has a prime power >
max k_i-1. One prime cannot so serve two tuples: their differing
offset difference is positive in absolute value and <=max k_i-1,
and is divisible by the smaller of the two powers. Restrict to all
tuples extending the prescribed partial assignment to get the count.
The conclusion is compatible with Siegel finiteness for fixed
columns. It provides effective restrictions when only selected
offsets are deficient in prime divisors. It does not bound the
heights of solutions when all offsets satisfy the restrictions.

2. Evaluated conditions for the two patterns at all heights

For each prime dividing a assign its full power to a term of greatest
valuation in each row. The following lists give the minimum number of
distinct assigned powers per factor n_k-s, in order s=0,...,k-1.
The counts are independent of the total number of primes dividing a.

    Columns (3,5,7,8)           Columns (5,6,7,8)
    k=3    9,9,9                k=5    6,6,6,6,6
    k=5    6,5,5,5,6            k=6    5,5,5,5,5,5
    k=7    4,4,4,4,4,4,4        k=7    4,4,4,4,4,4,4
    k=8    3,3,3,3,3,3,3,3      k=8    4,3,3,3,3,3,3,4

The separate finite host calculation in Erdos849HostCount.md strengthens every column-eight entry in this table to at least four.

Inputs. Erdos849PatternThree.py and Erdos849PatternSixHost.py check
the bound p^v_p(a)<=p^v_p(420) Y with Y=Q (a/10^128)^(1/36), Q=34800
or 35519, respectively. Erdos849PatternHeights.md excludes the two
patterns through a=10^E for E=136 or 144, respectively. The short
elementary prime-power exclusions in Erdos849PrimePowerRow.txt can
instead be used independently of those inputs but give weaker counts.

Let ell=lcm(1,...,k). The host cofactor b_s=(n_k-s)/(product of the
powers assigned to s) divides ell. For any prime p choose a maximum
term of valuation V and consider the count N_h of multiples of p^h
in the row. The exponent V-v_p(a) is sum_{h<=V} (1+floor(k/p^h)-N_h):
there are no further factorial levels since p^(V+1)>k. Each summand
is 0 or 1, and is zero for p^h>k. The exponent is thus at most
floor(log_p k). Other terms cannot contain a power >k of the same
prime because two such multiples cannot fit in the row. This proves
the cofactor claim, including primes absent from a.

Call an assigned prime exceptional when its full power exceeds Y; its
base then belongs to {2,3,5,7}. Its power exceeds 7, so all the other
terms have p-valuation v_p(s-v) at offset v!=s. The exponent in the
cofactor is consequently v_p(k!/(s!(k-1-s)!))=v_p(k*binom(k-1,s)).
If T is the set of exceptional bases, an upper bound for b_s times the
possible losses is therefore

     ell * product_{p in T} p^(v_p(k*binom(k-1,s))-v_p(ell)+v_p(420)).

Take the maximum B of this expression over all subsets of at most t
exceptional bases when testing a slot with <=t total powers. Then
n_k-s<=B Y^t, padding missing ordinary powers with units if needed.
On writing a=10^E Z, Z>=1, the opposite bound is
n_k-s>=(floor((k!10^E)^(1/k))-s) Z^(1/k), since n_k^k>=k!a.
For kt<=36, it suffices to check

  (B Q^t)^36 10^((E-128)t) < (floor((k!10^E)^(1/k))-s)^36.

Erdos849PatternGeometry.py evaluates these inequalities by integer
arithmetic for every listed slot. An entry t+1 means that all counts
up to t were eliminated. This is a consequence of the earlier height
and local inequalities, supplemented by the cofactor calculation.

3. Absolute simplicity of the natural genus-three quotients

In the pattern (3,5,7,8), keep just columns 3 and 8, using x=n_3,
y=(2n_8-7)^2. In (5,6,7,8) keep just columns 6 and 8, using
x=(2n_6-5)^2 and the same y. The two rational plane quartics are

  (y-1)(y-9)(y-25)(y-49) = (2^8 8!/3!) x(x-1)(x-2),       K3,
  (y-1)(y-9)(y-25)(y-49) = 224 (x-1)(x-9)(x-25),          K6.

Both their Jacobians are absolutely simple. In particular neither
of these genus-three curves admits a nonconstant map to a genus-one
or genus-two curve over the algebraic closure: such a map would
induce a nonzero-dimensional proper abelian subvariety by pullback.
This concerns these two quotients specifically. The full four-column
curves can have further quotients unrelated to these two projections.

Here is an exact certificate. For K3 use p=11 and for K6 use p=17.
In each case the original row polynomials f_k and f_8 have disjoint
finite branch sets modulo p, checked by taking the gcd of their two
critical-value resultants in F_p[t]. The critical values of the
reflected polynomial are included in the branch values of the full
map because the square map is surjective over an algebraic closure.
The two partial derivatives cannot both vanish at a point of the
affine quartic. In the degree-four homogenization the unique point
at infinity [1:0:0] is smooth by its nonzero Z-derivative. These
quartics have good reduction at the indicated primes and genus three.

    curve    p     #F_p      #F_(p^2)     #F_(p^3)
    K3       11      20         128          1358
    K6       17      19         337          4876

The three point counts in each row determine the three leading
nonconstant Frobenius coefficients by Newton sums; the functional
equation supplies the remaining coefficients. The resulting monic
Frobenius polynomials in variable T are

    K3: T^6 + 8T^5 + 35T^4 + 118T^3 + 385T^2 + 968T + 1331,
    K6: T^6 +  T^5 + 24T^4 +  11T^3 + 408T^2 + 289T + 4913.

They are irreducible modulo 5 and 31, respectively, so in particular
over Q. The six complex Frobenius roots are therefore conjugate.
No ratio of two different roots is a root of unity. Indeed such a
ratio would have degree at most 6*5=30, by the Galois action on
ordered distinct pairs. If its order is b then phi(b)<=30 and hence
b<=1800 since phi(b)>=sqrt(b/2). For every b in this finite range
with phi(b)<=30, compute the degree-six polynomial with roots the
b-th powers of the six Frobenius roots modulo 1000003 and test its
gcd with its derivative. All gcds are one. The computation uses
only modular power sums and Newton identities. A repeated root in
characteristic zero would have given zero discriminant modulo this
auxiliary prime also.

Thus over every finite extension of the chosen residual field the
six powered Frobenius conjugates remain distinct. They form a single
Galois orbit, so the Frobenius polynomial over the extension remains
irreducible over Q. A positive-dimensional proper abelian subvariety
would be defined over some finite extension and would yield a proper
positive-degree Frobenius-polynomial factor there. The reductions are
therefore absolutely simple. This implies absolute simplicity in
characteristic zero: a hypothetical geometric isogeny decomposition
would be defined over a number field and give a nontrivial rational
idempotent in the geometric endomorphism algebra. Specialization of
endomorphisms is injective at good reduction, so this idempotent would
survive, contradicting absolute simplicity of the reduction. See also
B. Smith, Families of explicitly isogenous Jacobians of variable-
separated curves, LMS J. Comput. Math. 14 (2011), section 14, on
deducing absolute simplicity from a good absolutely simple reduction.
https://doi.org/10.1112/S1461157010000410

Erdos849PatternGeometryQuartics.py reproduces the finite-field counts
by exact enumeration over fields of orders p,p^2,p^3, counts the
single point at infinity, and checks irreducibility and every powered
polynomial. The helper files Erdos849SmallestCurve.py and
Erdos849ThreeFiveCurve.py must be in the same directory.
