An explicit interpolation bound for a common value in four binomial columns

If a >= 10^230000000000000 occurs in at least five admissible rows of
Pascal's triangle, then a has at least 840 distinct prime divisors.
All logarithms in the height estimates below are to base ten.
The size of the displayed exponent is 230 trillion.

1. Choosing columns and local offsets

Suppose there are at most W=839 distinct prime divisors. Kummer's theorem
gives p^v_p(a) <= n in every representation a=C(n,k), hence a<=n^W.
For k>=840, 2k<=n, and n>2^840 840!, the elementary bound
C(n,k)>=C(n,840)>=(n/2)^840/840!>n^W excludes this representation.
The stated height forces all rows above this cutoff. Thus k<=839.

Five rows require four distinct proper columns, say i<j<k<d. The
published classifications, Theorem 2.2 of Blokhuis, Brouwer and de
Weger (https://arxiv.org/pdf/1707.06893), give j>=5, k>=7 and
i*j*k*d>=840. The product minimum follows by checking tuples through
24 and observing that 2*5*7*d already suffices when d>=24.
Take the largest columns first until their product P reaches 840.
They give r=2, 3 or 4 selected columns k_i, maximum d, and P<=839^2.
For r=2, d<=P/7. For r=3, the largest pair has product <840, so
d<=119 and d<=P/(5*7). For r=4, the largest three have product <840,
so d<=23 and d<=P/(2*5*7).

For every full prime power p^e exactly dividing a, put s_i=n_i mod p^e
in each selected row. Then 0<=s_i<k_i. Indeed in the floor formula for
v_p(C(n_i,k_i)) exactly e levels contribute 1. At some level h>=e
we have n_i mod p^h < k_i mod p^h <= k_i, giving the claim on reduction
modulo p^e. There are at most W occupied tuples s of these offsets.

2. Polynomial interpolation on the fiber above zero

Work in R=Z[t,x_1,...,x_r]/((x_i)_(k_i)-k_i!t for i=1,...,r), where
(x)_k is a falling factorial. The products x^h, 0<=h_i<k_i, form a
free Z[t]-basis of size P by monic division in each variable.
The generic algebra over Q(t) is a field by the geometric irreducibility
proof for distinct normalized binomial columns in sections 1–3 of
Erdos849ReciprocalRunge.txt. This last property is needed for the
nonvanishing of the norm in section 4.

Put b=min(P,1680), M=ceil(2br/(b-W)), N=b(M-r), and K=WM. Then N>K.
Use any b of the basis monomials and all their multiples t^q for
0<=q<M-r as N unknown coefficients of a polynomial F. For every
occupied tuple s set

  B_s = product over i and 0<=v<k_i, v!=s_i of (x_i-v).

Impose B_s^M F=0 in R/t^M. These are integer linear conditions.
Over Q[t]/t^M the ring splits as P copies of Q[t]/t^M: at t=0 all roots
of each falling factorial are simple rational integers, and each root
lifts uniquely to the truncated power series ring. On all components
apart from s, B_s is divisible by t. On the s component it is a unit.
Thus these conditions have rank at most M per occupied tuple, hence at
most K in total. An integral normal form which vanishes after tensoring
with Q already vanishes in R/t^M because its monomial basis is free.

For coefficient bounds use the sum of absolute coefficients of a
normal form. Multiplication by x_i in the monic basis has operator
norm at most 2d!: each column either shifts a monomial or uses the
defining monic relation, whose lower coefficients have absolute sum
at most 2 k_i!. This follows by evaluating the unsigned falling
factorial at 1. Addition of a shift -v increases the bound to S=3d!.
Multiplication by t has norm 1; truncation only decreases the norm.
The entries of every integer constraint row therefore have absolute
value <= E=S^((M+1)rd), counting at most Mrd linear factors in B_s^M
and at most rd more in the original basis monomial.

Keep a rational basis of the integer constraint rows. Let
J=ceil(K/(N-K)) and C=(2NE+1)^J. On the coefficient cube {0,...,C}^N
there are at least (C+1)^N inputs and at most (2NEC+1)^K outputs.
The former is larger, since C^(N-K)>=(2NE+1)^K. Subtract two inputs
with the same output. This gives a nonzero F in the required kernel,
with integer coefficients bounded by C. Freeness of the monomial
basis ensures F is nonzero in R.

3. Evaluation at the putative solution

For the tuple belonging to p^e, the identity B_s^M F=t^M G_s in R
has integral G_s. At t=a, x_i=n_i it gives p^(eM) | B_s(n)^M F(n).
Set D=(d!)^r. If p^e>d, then for v!=s_i,
v_p(n_i-v)=v_p(s_i-v), since 0<|s_i-v|<p^e. The valuation of B_s(n)
is at most that of D: for each i the corresponding product of
differences has absolute value s_i!(k_i-1-s_i)!, which divides d!.
If p^e<=d then p^e divides D directly. Combining all p gives

  a^M | D^M F(a,n_1,...,n_r).

Every n_i <= (k_i!a)^(1/k_i)+k_i <= 2d a^(1/k_i). Every basis
monomial used has weight q+sum h_i/k_i < M-1. Thus
|D^M F(a,n)| <= D^M N C (2d)^(rd) a^(M-1).
For a>D^M N C (2d)^(rd), the integer F(a,n) must vanish.

4. Concluding with a nonzero univariate polynomial

The determinant of multiplication by F on the P-dimensional monic
basis is a nonzero integer polynomial H(t), by the field property.
On specializing at t=a, evaluation at the rational tuple n gives a
homomorphism to Q. The equality F(a,n)=0 makes the specialized F
a nonunit, so H(a)=0. Each matrix entry has polynomial coefficient
sum <= N C S^(rd). The determinant expansion gives

  ||H||_1 <= P! (N C S^(rd))^P.

Every nonzero integer root has absolute value at most ||H||_1:
divide off all powers of t, and apply the rational root theorem to
the remaining integer polynomial with nonzero constant term.

Erdos849GridHeight.py checks that both logarithmic height bounds are
strictly below 230,000,000,000,000. It uses integer upper bounds on
logarithms only. For each r it checks every 840<=P<1680 and then the
largest allowed P,d for P>=1680. In this last range b,M,N,K,J are
constant for each r. The largest upper bound occurs for two selected
columns at P=840. This proves the stated restriction, leaving the
existence of any five-row value unresolved.

5. An explicit restriction on each individual numerator factor

For the fixed columns (3,5,7,8), begin directly at section 2 with
P=840, d=8, r=4. That part of the proof uses only the number of
occupied tuples, regardless of how many primes map to each tuple.
The upper bound on log(a) with at most 839 occupied tuples is
1,818,901,158,481. Thus for a>=10^1820000000000, every one of the
840 tuples must receive a full prime power. Distinct tuples receive
different prime bases. Holding a single coordinate s_i fixed, the
840/k_i tuples extending it therefore give 840/k_i distinct primes
which divide both a and n_i-s_i. In particular each of the eight
consecutive numerator factors in column 8 shares at least 105
distinct primes with a. The corresponding counts for columns 3, 5
and 7 are 280, 168 and 120. No bound on omega(a) is assumed here.
