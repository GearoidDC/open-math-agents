# A direct restriction on the common value modulo 113

If $a\le 10^{175}$ occurs in columns $5,6,7,8$ of admissible rows, then $a\equiv0$ or $1\pmod{113}$. In particular this is necessary throughout the remaining interval for a candidate with exactly 40 prime divisors. The residue calculation itself makes no assumption about the number of primes. [Prime-count thresholds](Erdos849ConditionalEntropy.md)

In column order $(8,7,6,5)$, direct enumeration in $\mathbb F_{113}$ gives just five common coefficient residues, namely $0,1,65,73,98$. The verifier excludes the last three. There are six corresponding row tuples after reflecting the two even-column coordinates independently by $X\mapsto k-1-X$. This reflection preserves the falling factorial in even degree. An admissible positive row or its reflected negative row has absolute value at most $(k!10^{175})^{1/k}+k$.

For a tuple, take a coordinate $X_f$ as free and write $X_f=s_f+113T$. All other falling-factorial derivatives are nonzero at their recorded offsets modulo 113. The other rows consequently lift uniquely in their residue classes for each $T$. More explicitly, lift at $T=0$ and expand the row equations around those 113-adic roots. The coefficient of each successive power of $X_f-s_f$ in a dependent row is determined by division by its unit derivative. The resulting power series has coefficients in $\mathbb Z_{113}$. Substituting $113T$ shows that, modulo $113^m$, an integer polynomial in the rows restricts to a polynomial in $T$ of degree at most $m-1$ with 113-adic integer coefficients. Its values at $T=0,\ldots,m-1$ determine every integer value by the Newton difference formula.

Each record supplies explicit auxiliary polynomials $F$. The verifier evaluates them on those $m$ lifts by modular Newton iteration. All evaluations are zero modulo $113^m$. Independently it bounds $|F|$ above by the sum over monomials of the absolute coefficient times the product of the integer row bounds. That sum is strictly less than $113^m$, so every $F$ would vanish at the actual integer rows. At a second small prime the program lists every tuple satisfying the four row equations. Each such tuple fails at least one equation $F=0$. This rules out the entire original residue class.

Integral lattice reduction provided candidate polynomials for the certificate. Verification uses only the Python standard library, and checks every class, coefficient bound, lift and finite-field exclusion. Run `python -B Erdos849ResidueDisks.py` in the research directory.
