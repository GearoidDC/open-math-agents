Fourteen distinct prime factors are necessary for five rows of Pascal's triangle

Let M(a) count the integers n for which C(n,k)=a with 1≤k and 2k≤n.
Let ω(a) be the number of distinct prime divisors of a. We obtain

    M(a) ≥ 5  implies  a > 10^100 and ω(a) ≥ 14.

The result leaves open the existence of even one such integer. It combines
published classifications, ordinary number theory, Lean proofs and two
independent integer computations. This note specifies the inputs and gives
enough detail to reproduce the deductions. We make no claim of priority.

1. Finite base and column bounds

The published Theorem 2.2 of Blokhuis, Brouwer and de Weger classifies
collisions up to 10^60 and in nine fixed pairs of columns. The enclosed
finite-search programs extend the five-row exclusion through 10^100.
The details and commands are in Erdos849-googol-note.txt and
Erdos849-reproduce.txt. See https://arxiv.org/pdf/1707.06893 .

Suppose a>10^100 and ω(a)≤13. In any admissible representation C(n,d)=a
the largest power of each prime dividing a is at most n. Indeed, for each
p choose a numerator term n-s of largest p-adic valuation. The valuation
of every other n-i is at most that of |s-i|, since p^v_p(n-i) divides
both n-i and n-s. Their sum of valuations is at most v_p(s!(d-1-s)!),
which is at most v_p(d!). Thus P=p^v_p(a) divides n-s and a≤n^13.
In any second representation there is likewise an r<j with P dividing m-r.
One may take the least nonnegative residues s=n mod P and r=m mod P.

For X=10^100, put N=floor(X^(1/13)). The integer comparison
C(N,15)>N^13 and the increasing ratio C(n,15)/n^13 for n≥N rule out
all columns d≥15 by monotonicity along the left half of the row. The
same test at X=10^390 rules out all d≥14. Each fixed positive column
can supply at most one row, since C(n,d) strictly increases with n.

2. A local obstruction at every prime

Assign each maximal prime power P to one divisible numerator term of
C(n,d). Divide each n-s by its assigned powers and call the resulting
positive integer b_s. Coprimality of the assigned powers guarantees
integrality. The exact identity is

    product over s of b_s = d!.

Each b_s divides d!. If e slots are empty, at least 2(d-e)-13 slots
contain a single prime power, since each other occupied slot contains
at least two. At a single slot write n-s=bP, so b=b_s. This description
does not require that the base prime be larger than either column.

Suppose C(n,d)=C(m,j)=a, j<d. Expand j! times the falling product at n,
and d! times the falling product at m. In each product one selected term
is divisible by P. The other factors can therefore be reduced modulo P
when computing modulo P². This gives divisibility by P² of

    Δ = (-1)^(d-1-s) A(n-s) - (-1)^(j-1-r) B(m-r),
    A = j! s! (d-1-s)!,         B = d! r! (j-1-r)!.

The two signs matter. If they agree and the two terms might be equal,
the verifier permits b=1. Otherwise Δ is nonzero after the indicated
size comparison. Divide Δ by the p-free part g of gcd(A,B). The quotient
is still an integer multiple of P².

3. Computing cofactor bounds for infinitely many values

For a≥X set y=a/X and R_k=floor((k!X)^(1/k)). Comparing a falling
product with its largest and smallest term shows, for C(u,k)=a, h<k,

    (R_k-h)y^(1/k) ≤ u-h ≤ (R_k+k-h)y^(1/k).

For a pair j<d<2j and any s,r, put L=A(R_d+d-s), U=B(R_j+j-r).
For equal signs, B(R_j-r)>L guarantees nonvanishing at every y≥1,
since 1/j>1/d. Use H=max(L,U)/g in that case. For opposite signs use
H=(L+U)/g. Then P²≤H y^(1/j). The identity n-s=bP gives

    b² H ≥ (R_d-s)² y^(2/d-1/j) ≥ (R_d-s)².

All constants are integers. For each s and base-prime class the program
finds the smallest possible divisor b of d! consistent with these
inequalities for at least one r. All primes p>d have the same bound
because none divides A or B. Each small prime may occur at only one
singleton slot. A dynamic program finds the least product allowed for
the required number of singleton slots, trying every assignment of these
prime classes. Each empty slot additionally contributes at least R_d-d+1.
Any resulting product greater than d! contradicts the exact identity.

For X=10^390 the procedure excludes every 7≤j<d≤13. At most one column
in {2,3,4,6} can occur by the published results. Adding the edge and
column 5 still leaves any five-row value needing two distinct columns ≥7.
This excludes the whole unbounded range a≥10^390 for ω(a)≤13.

4. The remaining interval

For 10^100≤a≤10^390, columns 2 through 14 split into three groups,

    {2,3,4,6},       {5,7,8},       {9,10,11,12,13,14}.

All pairs within the first group have published classifications. For
the other two groups the preceding cofactor test either excludes a pair
outright or shows that its larger-column row has an empty slot or a
singleton slot of small prime base p≤d. Column 14 must have an empty
slot because there are at most 13 distinct prime factors.

An empty slot has n=b+s with b dividing d!. A small-prime singleton
has n=b*p^h+s with b dividing d!, 1≤h, p≤d prime, 0≤s<d. In this
bounded interval every possible row of either type can be enumerated
directly. There is no search over all integers a in the interval.
For a prime l>d, Lucas's congruence implies C(n,d) mod l depends only
on n mod l. If this residue is absent from {C(u,j) mod l: 0≤u<l},
there is no equality with any row of the smaller column.

    Larger column    Smaller columns checked       Candidate rows
            7        5                                    46,459
            8        5                                    67,368
           13        12                                1,215,500
           14        7 through 13                          3,094
                                                       ---------
                                                       1,332,421

All these candidates were excluded by modular arithmetic. The second
verifier generated rows using p-free divisors, computed the exact
binomial coefficients, and used a disjoint set of modular primes.
Both implementations independently checked the column graph. Each
of the three groups contributes at most one row. Adding the edge
gives at most four, proving the claim.

5. Scope of verification

Erdos849FourteenGraph.lean verifies the column cutoff, the coloring
argument and the deduction of the fourteen-prime conclusion from the
published pairs, finite base and the specified middle- and upper-range
pair exclusions. The numerical pair exclusions are verified by Python.
The files on hosts and sparse collisions check the underlying assignment
and congruence in Lean. A separate Lean theorem deduces eleven distinct
prime factors directly from the finite base and the classifications,
without the two additional numerical pair certificates.

The finite collision search, both fourteen-prime verifiers, the Lean
modules and their rebuild instructions are included in this packet.
