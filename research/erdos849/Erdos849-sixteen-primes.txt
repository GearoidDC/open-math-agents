Five rows in Pascal's triangle require at least sixteen distinct primes

Write M(a) for the number of rows n with C(n,k)=a for some k≥1, 2k≤n.
Every five-row value must satisfy both

    a > 10^100,      ω(a) ≥ 16,

where ω(a) counts distinct prime divisors. This is a necessary condition.
The existence of a value with M(a)≥5 remains open.

Proof

The base range through 10^100 is covered by Theorem 2.2 of Blokhuis,
Brouwer and de Weger up to 10^60 and the exact finite searches described
in Erdos849-reproduce.txt up to 10^100. The published theorem also excludes
nine fixed pairs of columns at larger values. Its classification and the
Lind–Singmaster family appear at https://arxiv.org/pdf/1707.06893 .

1. Reducing an infinite search to four columns.

Suppose M(a)≥5 and ω(a)≤15. A fixed positive column increases strictly
down its admissible rows. Aside from the edge k=1 there must be at least
four different proper columns. Choose any four, written in increasing order.

In any row a=C(n,d) each full prime power P=p^v_p(a) divides at least one
term n-s, 0≤s<d. One proof selects a term with maximum p-adic valuation.
For every other term n-i, its p-part divides |s-i|. The product of those
differences is s!(d-1-s)!, a divisor of d!. This proves the assertion.
In particular a≤n^15. The integer checks C(N,t)>N^15 with
N=floor(X^(1/15)) give t=18 at X=10^100 and t=16 at X=10^500.
The ratio C(n,t)/n^15 increases on n≥N since t>15. Monotonicity across
the half-row excludes all columns ≥t. Thus there are only 1,820 four-column
subsets to consider above a googol and 1,001 above 10^500.

2. Lower bounds on the product of cofactors.

Assign each P to one term n-s that it divides. Divide each term by all
its assigned powers and call the resulting integer b_s. Then

    product_s b_s = d!.

If e terms have no assigned powers, at least S=max(0,2(d-e)-15) terms
have exactly one. This follows by counting powers across the d-e nonempty
terms. Each empty term has b_s=n-s. Each singly occupied term has n-s=b_s P.

For another representation a=C(m,j), let a=Xy, y≥1, and put
R_k=floor((k!X)^(1/k)). A row u in column k satisfies

    (R_k-h)y^(1/k) ≤ u-h ≤ (R_k+k-h)y^(1/k),   0≤h<k.

This follows by bounding the k-term falling product by its extreme terms.
In particular each empty term contributes at least L=R_d-d+1 to d!.

There are two complementary bounds on the product of the S single-slot
cofactors. For the first, assign their pairwise-coprime prime powers
to divisible terms in the other row. If h powers are assigned to a single
m-r, their product divides m-r. Hence the product of their cofactors
is at least L^h/(R_j+j) times y^(h/d-1/j). For hj≥d, round L^h/(R_j+j)
up to an integer and use at least one. For hj<d use one. The program
minimizes the product over all ways of distributing S powers into j groups.
This yields a uniform lower bound for every a≥X.

For the second bound, given n-s=b_s P and P|(m-r), the falling-product
identity implies P² divides the signed difference

    (-1)^(d-1-s) A(n-s) - (-1)^(j-1-r) B(m-r),
    A=j!s!(d-1-s)!,       B=d!r!(j-1-r)!.

This follows by reducing the other factors modulo P. When the difference
is nonzero, divide by the p-free part of gcd(A,B). The resulting absolute
value remains at least P². For j<d<2j the row estimates above yield a
lower bound on b_s depending only on X, j,d,s and the base-prime class.
The test uses the trivial bound one whenever it cannot rule out a zero
difference. It rounds bounds up to divisors of d!, tries every possible r
and assigns each small prime base at most once. The full inequalities
and the cofactor-product computation are in Erdos849-prime-bounds.txt
and Erdos849GroupedHosts.txt.

For each feasible e, multiply the stronger of these two product bounds
by L^e. If it exceeds d!, the pair is impossible. The program can also
repeat the test assuming all singly occupied terms have base p>d. If only
this last test succeeds, any surviving row must contain a singleton term
of small prime base or an empty term. An empty term has n=b+s, b dividing
d!. A singleton of small prime base has n=b p^h+s, p≤d prime, h≥1 and
b dividing d!. In a bounded value interval, all these rows are explicitly
enumerable.

3. The upper range and the bounded remainder.

At X=10^500 the column-pair obstructions meet all 1,001 four-column
subsets in columns 2 through 15. They apply at all larger values.
These checks with ω(a)≤15 use no special theorem about (7,14).

For 10^100≤a≤10^500, apply the same tests to all subsets of four proper
columns through 17. For most subsets they either exclude a pair or reduce
it to the finite small-prime and empty-term rows above. For each of the
18 other subsets choose a larger X=10^K where at least one of its pairs
passes such a test. The following table lists their finite gaps. Each
comma-separated group of four numbers denotes a set of columns.

    K      Four proper columns
    101    (2,7,14,15), (3,5,14,15), (3,7,14,15), (5,7,14,15)
    104    (3,5,7,11), (4,5,7,11), (5,6,7,11)
    111    (3,5,8,9)
    121    (3,5,7,14), (4,5,7,14), (5,6,7,14)
    134    (3,5,7,13), (4,5,7,13), (5,6,7,13)
    144    (3,5,7,12), (4,5,7,12), (5,6,7,12)
    179    (3,5,7,15)

For every listed set, sieve all rows in its largest column d whose value
is in (10^100,10^K]. For any prime l>d, C(n,d) mod l depends only on
n mod l and must belong to the residue images of all three other columns.
The Python program intersects periodic bitmaps along residue progressions.
The Node program independently joins residue classes using the Chinese
remainder theorem. Both derive closed integer row endpoints and use exact
integer comparisons. No modular survivor occurs in any of the 18 intervals.

The separate exceptional-row checks enumerate n=b+s or n=b p^h+s at the
possible larger columns d=8,9,11,12,13,14,15,16,17. The modular test for
the corresponding smaller column excludes every row. Removing all these
cases rules out the assumed four proper columns. This proves the bound.

Verification boundary

Erdos849SixteenGraph.lean proves the two column cutoffs and the final row
count deduction conditional on the published pair results, the finite
base and the computed four-column certificate. The numerical certificates
run outside Lean. The generic prime-power congruence and an explicit
height bound for collisions with ω(a)<2j are separately proved in Lean.
The scripts and full rebuild commands are in Erdos849-verification.txt.

Further restriction above 10^524

Repeating the column tests at X=10^500 with ω(a)≤16 leaves only three
potential four-column sets: (3,5,7,8), (5,6,7,8) and (3,5,7,15).
For the first two sets consider just columns 7 and 8, now at X=10^524.
The signed-square inequality in step 2 gives P≤Q_p y^(1/14) for each
full prime power P. The constants Q_p are computed by maximizing over
the finitely many offset pairs, checking that the signed difference
cannot vanish, and removing the p-free common divisor as above.

Write Q=max Q_p and L=R_8-7. The test L>8!Q makes a slot with zero or
one assigned power impossible. Since ω(a)≤16, every one of the eight
slots has exactly two powers. At least three of these sixteen powers
must divide one of the seven numerator terms in the other row. If they
come from three distinct original slots, the product of their three
cofactors divides 8!, and their three partner powers have distinct
prime bases. Their product therefore has lower bound

    L³ y^(9/56) / (8! times the product of the three partner constants).

If two of the three came from the same slot, use their exact product
(n-s)/b_s, giving a lower bound L² y^(10/56)/(8!Q). Both exceed the
upper bound (R_7+7)y^(1/7) for the receiving term, by the explicit
integer comparisons in Erdos849SeventeenLarge.py.

For the third set, columns 7 and 15 already conflict at X=10^188.
Make a bipartite graph with a vertex for every numerator term in the
two rows, and one edge for each prime, joining its assigned terms in
the two representations. Every vertex is occupied at this height.
With 22 vertices and at most 16 edges, there are at least six connected
components. At least five each have only one vertex on the seven-term
side. Within each component the prime-power products are identical on
both sides. Comparing the term products is therefore equivalent to
comparing their cofactor products. Write L_k=R_k-k+1 and U_k=R_k+k.
The inequalities L_15³>15! U_7 and 3/15>1/7 forbid three or more
15-side vertices in any of the five components. With at most two such
vertices, L_7>5 U_15² and 1/7>2/15 force the lone seven-side cofactor
to exceed five. Five integer cofactors ≥6 would give 7!≥6^5, impossible.
Erdos849ComponentBound.py checks these constants. The resulting bound
is ω(a)≥17 for all a≥10^524 with five admissible rows.
