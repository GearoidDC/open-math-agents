At least eighteen distinct primes at height 10^524

Suppose a=C(n,d)=C(m,j), j<d, a=X y, X=10^524, y>=1, and write w
for the number of distinct prime divisors. Assign each full prime
power p^e exactly dividing a to the term n-s with s=n mod p^e.
The argument in Erdos849GridHeight.txt gives s<d. Assign it likewise
on the j side. The quotient of each term by its assigned powers is
a positive integer, and the products of these cofactors are d! and
j!, respectively. Write R_k=floor((k!X)^(1/k)), L_k=R_k-k+1 and
U_k=R_k+k. Each term in the k-column row lies between
L_k y^(1/k) and U_k y^(1/k), by comparing the extreme factors.

1. An additional obstruction for (7,8)

The signed congruence and integer bounds in Erdos849SeventeenLarge.py
bound every assigned power P by Q_p y^(1/14), including powers which
share their original slot with others. Indeed modulo P^2, each falling
product is its unique chosen host term times the signed product of
the offset differences from that host. Cross-multiplication gives a
signed linear expression divisible by P^2. Nonzero magnitude and
the p-free common multiplier are checked over the 56 possible
offset pairs. The new verifier also bounds all Q_p by one constant Q.

At X the eight terms each need at least two assigned powers, because
L_8>8! Q and 1/8>1/14. Call the powers from slots containing exactly
two powers ordinary. No three ordinary powers can belong to a single
seven-side term. To verify this, if they come from distinct paired
slots, multiply their three lower bounds obtained by dividing the
three slot sizes by their cofactors and partner powers. The three
cofactors have product at most 8!, and the three partners have
distinct prime bases. This lower bound exceeds U_7 y^(1/7) since
3/8-3/14>1/7 and the integer inequality holds at X. If two come
from one pair, its product is the slot size divided by its cofactor.
Only the third power has to be divided by a partner. The resulting
exponent 2/8-1/14 again exceeds 1/7. These are the distinct- and
shared-pair comparisons in eight_seven(524); their proof uses only
the ordinary powers in question.

If w=16 there are sixteen ordinary powers and only seven receiving
terms, which is impossible. If w=17 there are seven pairs and one
triple. The fourteen ordinary powers distribute exactly two to each
seven-side term. At least four of those terms contain none of the
triple's powers. Their eight ordinary powers occupy only seven
original pair slots, so include a complete original pair.

If the two powers of that pair come from one of the four receiving
terms, their product is at least (L_7/7!)y^(1/7). If from different
ones, divide the product of the two receiving terms by their
cofactors and other powers. Their two cofactors multiply to a divisor
of 7!, so the product is at least (L_7^2/(7! Q^2))y^(1/7).
Both expressions exceed U_8 y^(1/8) by exact comparison at X.
The complete pair divides one eight-side term, giving a contradiction.

2. Component exclusions and the remaining column patterns

Use a bipartite graph on all j+d terms with one edge per prime,
joining its two hosts. For the pairs tested L_j>j! and L_d>d!
exclude isolated vertices. A connected component has h>=1 vertices
on the d side and q>=1 on the j side. The products of the assigned
powers agree, so the ratio of the two term products is D/J for some
positive D|d!, J|j!. Compare the lower and upper row bounds at X,
retaining an exclusion throughout y>=1 only when the lower side
grows at least as fast in y. If h/d=q/j, then for some J|j! an
integer D must lie in the closed interval

  J L_d^h/U_j^q <= D <= J U_d^h/L_j^q.

The verifier discards impossible (h,q) pairs. A graph with w<=17
edges has at least j+d-17 components. A finite recurrence checks
whether the sum of that many or more allowed pairs can equal (d,j).
It excludes the column pair when there is no such partition.

Finally the usual a<=n^17 bound and the comparison C(n,18)>n^17 at
the lower row limit force every proper column to be at most 17.
The comparison persists with increasing n by writing C(n,18)/n^17
as (n/18!) times the product of (1-h/n), h=0,...,17.
There are 1,820 four-element subsets of columns 2 through 17.
The earlier published and computed pair exclusions, the new component
test and the (7,8) obstruction together meet all of them.
Run python Erdos849EighteenPrimes.py to verify the integer inequalities
and exhaust the column sets. Its output is an empty unresolved list.
