# Prime assignments for columns five and seven

Every `a = C(n,5) = C(m,7) ≥ 10^136`, with `n ≥ 5` and `m ≥ 7`, has at least 35 distinct prime factors. Assign each full prime power dividing `a` to a largest-valuation term of each falling factorial. The position pair `(i,j)` refers to `n−i` and `m−j`, with indices starting at zero. The certificates below establish that all 35 position pairs are occupied. Each prime gets one position pair, so the 35 witnesses are distinct.

To replay the result with standard Python, run `python -B Erdos849FiveSevenBranches.py --all-cells`. The verifier checks 18 families and their reflections, giving 5,060 branch and position combinations across all 35 cells. The largest interval radius is 9,887,158. Every branch closes by the real bounds or by an exhaustive modular sieve.

`Erdos849FiveSevenCells.py` and its adjacent JSON verify that all 35 pairs are occupied whenever `a ≥ 10^400`. For each cell they calculate a denominator `D` for which a missing pair implies `Z = D a / ((n−i)(m−j))` is an integer. The denominator computation and slope bounds come from `Erdos849SaturatedCells.py`. With `x=n` and `y=m−j`, the certificate reduces an integral combination of `binom(Z,t)` by `42 (x)_5 = (y+j)_7`. Its regular polynomial part `P` takes integral values on the full integer grid. The difference `F` is an integer expressed as a Laurent polynomial with only negative weighted degrees `7u+5v`. Absolute values and the slope bounds give a finite interval for `F`. The verifier either shows `0<|F|<1` or excludes the remaining integers by images over good finite fields. Some of the supplied certificates already work at `10^272`.

For the real bounds, write `T = y^(1/5)`. The certified constants give `T ≥ B` and `lo*T^7 < x < hi*T^7`. Every remainder monomial `x^u y^v` has `0 ≤ u < 5` and strictly negative weight `7u+5v`. The weights are distinct because 5 and 7 are coprime. When the lower bound on the largest-weight term at `B` exceeds the sum of the upper bounds on all other terms there, it keeps that sign for every `T ≥ B`. After division by the leading power of `T`, each remaining upper bound decreases with `T`.

## Certificates on smooth residue classes

`Erdos849FiveSevenBranches.py`, its compressed JSON and `Erdos849FiveSevenHenselAudit.py` verify earlier thresholds for the cells recorded in that JSON. The verifier checks the original cell and its reflection separately. The coordinate involution `(n,m) ↦ (4−n,6−m)` sends `Z` to `−Z` for the reflected cell. The verifier applies the binomial identity for `binom(−Z,t)` and recomputes the integral, real and modular bounds for the resulting coefficients.

Let `p` be a prime larger than both columns and the degree in `Z`. Suppose `(n,m)` reduces to `(n₀,m₀)` modulo `p`. The residue `(i,j)` is impossible for a missing pair, since `5040 (n−i) Z = D ∏_{s≠j}(m−s)` has nonzero right side at this residue. The verifier enumerates every remaining point of the finite-field curve. On each point, one partial derivative of the curve equation is a unit. If the derivative in `m` is a unit, write `n=n₀+pT` and vary `m` by implicit lifting. Otherwise interchange the coordinates. The implicit expansion for the dependent coordinate in the displacement `pT` has coefficients in `Z_p`. This follows by solving for each successive coefficient using the unit derivative, starting at the unique base lift. One of `m−j` and `n−i` is a unit. The corresponding polynomial identity expresses `Z` as a regular function on this branch. All rational coefficients in the regular part of the certificate have denominators prime to `p`.

Consequently `F` modulo `p^E` is a polynomial in `T` of degree at most `E−1`. The audit evaluates the original expression `Σ c_t binom(Z,t)−P` on the first `E` integer inputs, with digitwise Hensel lifts, and checks divisibility by `p^E` at every input. The Newton difference formula then implies the divisibility for all integral `T`. Thus `F/p^E` is an integer on the branch. Dividing the real bound by `p^E` gives an interval for this integer. Division by `p^E` modulo every other good prime gives the residue images used to sieve that interval. Prime-field evaluation includes row residues `m=j` using the identity with denominator `n−i`.

Both programs run with standard Python and assertions enabled. The branch verifier accepts an optional path to another compressed certificate in the same format.
