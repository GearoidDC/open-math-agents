Bitangent descent and geometric endomorphisms of the two quartic quotients

Take the smooth projective plane quartics K3,K6 of Erdos849PatternGeometry.txt,
with affine equations G(y)=cH(x). In both cases G(y)=(y-1)(y-9)(y-25)(y-49).
For K3, H(x)=x(x-1)(x-2) and c=1720320. For K6, H(x)=(x-1)(x-9)(x-25)
and c=224. Their Jacobians have rational Mordell-Weil ranks at least 8 and
7, respectively. Both geometric endomorphism rings equal Z. In particular
their geometric Picard numbers are exactly one. These results concern the
genus-three quotients and do not classify points lifting to admissible rows.

1. Bitangents and rank

Each curve has a rational hyperflex I=[1:0:0]; its tangent Z=0 intersects
the quartic in 4I. If L:Y-mX-bZ=0 is a bitangent in good odd characteristic,
div(L/Z)=2D-4I, with both terms defined over the residue field. Evaluation
on a degree-zero divisor avoiding the support induces a homomorphism from
J(F_p)/2J(F_p) to F_p^*/F_p^{*2}. Indeed principal divisors map to squares
by Weil reciprocity, since the divisor of L/Z is even. Rational degree-zero
classes may be moved away from the support, and the rational point I identifies
the degree-zero Picard group with the rational Jacobian. Compose with good
reduction to get a homomorphism on J(Q)/2J(Q). This use of bitangents as a
descent pairing is described in Bruin, Poonen and Stoll, section 12.
https://math.mit.edu/~poonen/papers/genus3.pdf

The explicit points are the twelve points with x a root of H and y a root
of G (x first and y second, both in increasing order), then these three
additional points [X:Y:Z]:

   K3  [3:81:1],  [11:225:1],  [-1:1203:27]
   K6  [49:81:1], [529:441:1], [273:273:1].

All satisfy the equations over Q. Subtract the first split point P0 from
each of the other fourteen points. At each chosen local bitangent the
evaluation of P-P0 is the square class of (L(P)/Z(P))/(L(P0)/Z(P0)).
All denominators and evaluations are nonzero at the selected primes.

Each line below records (p,m,b,mask). The fourteen bits of mask, from the
least significant upward, are the nonsquareness indicators in the stated
order of point differences.

To verify a line, substitute y=mx+b and check that the resulting quartic
is m^4(x^2+ux+v)^2. The two middle unknowns u,v follow successively from
the cubic and quadratic terms. The projective restriction has no zeros
at infinity because m is nonzero. Each prime has good reduction by the
finite branch resultant test of Erdos849PatternGeometryQuartics.py and
smoothness at I. Binary row reduction gives ranks nine and seven. Since
#J_K3(F11)=2846 has 2-adic valuation one, its rational 2-torsion has
dimension at most one by injectivity of prime-to-p torsion reduction.
#J_K6(F17)=5647 is odd. Subtracting these upper bounds on 2-torsion gives
the claimed Mordell-Weil rank bounds.

There is no second rational bitangent on either curve. Modulo 13 for K3
and 17 for K6, exhaustive testing finds no affine bitangent of slope
m!=0; the script checks all m,b. A horizontal line contains I simply.
On a vertical line, the identity

      G(y)=(y^2-42y+105)^2-4096y

prevents G(y)-constant from being a square up to scale in those two
characteristics: its leading three coefficients force the displayed
quadratic, but its linear coefficient then disagrees. Thus Z=0 is the
only residue-field rational bitangent. Distinct rational bitangents
specialize distinctly at either good odd prime. Indeed their half-contact
divisors represent distinct theta characteristics (an effective degree-two
divisor on a nonhyperelliptic curve cannot move in a pencil). A coincidence
on specialization would kill their nonzero 2-torsion difference, which is
impossible by good reduction. The rank evaluation above deliberately uses
bitangents available only over the respective finite fields.

2. Geometric endomorphism rings

For each curve use the following good primes p. In the table q is a prime
modulo which its degree-six monic Frobenius polynomial F is irreducible.
If the coefficients following T^6 in F are A,B,C,pB,p^2 A,p^3, then
F(T)=T^3 g(T+p/T), with g=U^3+A U^2+(B-3p)U+C-2pA. Its discriminant
is shown in the last column.

        p      #K(F_p), #K(F_p^2), #K(F_p^3)    A   B    C     q     disc(g)
  K3   11       20,128,1358                      8  35   118    5       11476
       13       21,189,2205                      7  34   126   31       29165
  K6   17       19,337,4876                      1  24    11   31       76448
       19       32,408,6626                     12  95   486    7        3028

As in Erdos849PatternGeometry.txt, for each p the checker proves that no
ratio of two distinct Frobenius roots is a root of unity. It tests the
separability of the polynomial on the b-th powers of the roots modulo
1000003 for every b with phi(b)<=30. A ratio's degree cannot exceed 30
because a sextic has only thirty ordered pairs of distinct roots.
Injectivity of endomorphism action on the prime-to-p rational Tate module
therefore bounds the dimension of the geometric endomorphism algebra in
characteristic p by six. More precisely, its endomorphisms are collectively
defined over a finite extension; they commute with the corresponding power
of Frobenius, whose six distinct eigenvalues give a six-dimensional
centralizer. Since Q[Frobenius] already has degree six, the geometric
endomorphism algebra in characteristic p equals E_p=Q[T]/(F).

For each curve take the first listed prime. The splitting group of F
permutes the three pairs {z,p/z} of complex-conjugate roots. The field
Q(z+p/z) has degree three because Q(z) has degree six and z is quadratic
over it. The cubic discriminant is nonsquare, so the projected splitting
group is S3. At auxiliary primes 79 (K3) and 61 (K6), the squarefree
sextic has exactly four linear roots, respectively {12,47,50,65} and
{8,24,25,49}. The factor degrees 1,1,1,1,2 give a transposition in the
characteristic-zero Galois group. A transposition preserving the partition
into pairs must flip exactly one pair. Its conjugates under the action
on the three blocks independently flip each pair. Thus the group is the
full signed permutation group (C2)^3 semidirect S3.

In this action the point stabilizer fixes its marked sheet and its pair,
and permits all flips and permutations of the two other pairs. An
overgroup either induces S3 on pairs or lies in the marked pair
stabilizer. In the former case conjugation provides every single flip,
and the overgroup is the entire group. In the latter case the pair
stabilizer has index two over the point stabilizer. By Galois
correspondence the only proper nontrivial subfield of this first sextic
E_p is its real cubic R_p=Q(z+p/z).

Good reduction injects the rational geometric endomorphism algebra in
characteristic zero into E_p at both good primes for each curve (see also
the specialization argument and source in Erdos849PatternGeometry.txt).
If this subalgebra exceeded Q it would contain a copy of the first real
cubic. In the second sextic E_q every totally real subfield is contained
in its real cubic R_q: under a complex embedding, conjugation exchanges
z and q/z and its fixed subfield is exactly R_q. An embedding R_p->E_q
would therefore identify the two cubic fields. Their polynomial
discriminants have different square classes (the product in each pair
in the table is a nonsquare), excluding that identification. The
endomorphism algebra is consequently Q, and its integral order is Z.
For a principally polarized Jacobian, the Neron--Severi group tensored
with Q corresponds to its Rosati-symmetric rational endomorphisms;
hence the geometric Picard number is one in both cases.

The standard numerical criterion for Chabauty--Coleman requires rational
rank <g. The usual quadratic Chabauty sufficient rank criterion is
r<g+rho-1 (Balakrishnan--Dogra, Lemma 3.2). Here g=3 and rho=1, while
r>=8 or r>=7. Neither sufficient condition holds. The bitangent tests
provide lower bounds, not a completed Selmer-group calculation or a new
height bound on admissible binomial collisions.
https://kclpure.kcl.ac.uk/portal/files/149873874/rationalpoints_heights_with_biblio.pdf

uses the earlier local helper files Erdos849PatternGeometryQuartics.py,
Erdos849SmallestCurve.py and Erdos849ThreeFiveCurve.py.
