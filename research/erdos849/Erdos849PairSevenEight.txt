Eighteen distinct primes in a value shared by columns seven and eight

Let a=C(n,8)=C(m,7)>=X=10^128 be in the left half of both rows.
Then a has at least eighteen distinct prime divisors.

For k=7,8, write L_k and U_k for the lower and upper factor bounds
at X, as in Erdos849EighteenPrimes.txt. Put y=a/X. Each numerator term
of column k lies in [L_k y^(1/k), U_k y^(1/k)]. Each full prime
power of a is assigned to one term of each row and divides that term.
Dividing each term by its assigned powers leaves an integer cofactor.
The product of the cofactors on the k side is k!.

The certificates in Erdos849LocalInterpolation.txt give each power
the bound p^v_p(420) Q y^(1/16), Q=46862860, and already give at least
seventeen primes. Suppose there are exactly seventeen. Every term
on the 8 side needs at least two assigned powers, since L_8>8! 7Q.
Thus seven terms receive two powers each, and one term receives three.
Call the fourteen powers in pairs ordinary.

No term on the 7 side receives three ordinary powers. If they come
from three different pairs, their product is at least the product
of the three 8-side terms divided by the three cofactors and the
three partner powers. It exceeds the upper 7-side bound because

  L_8^3 > 8! 420 Q^3 U_7,     3/8-3/16 >= 1/7.

If two of them form a complete pair, the two originating terms give
the same contradiction using L_8^2 > 8! 7 Q U_7 and 2/8-1/16 >= 1/7.
Exactly two ordinary powers therefore go to each of the seven
receiving terms. At least four of these terms receive no further power.
The eight ordinary powers in any four such terms come from only seven
pairs, so include a complete pair.

If this pair occupies two different receiving terms, multiply those
terms and divide by their cofactors and the two remaining powers.
Their losses p^v_p(420) have product at most 35, since the bases are
distinct. The resulting pair product exceeds a whole 8-side term by

  L_7^2 > 7! 35 Q^2 U_8,      2/7-2/16 >= 1/8,

contradicting the assignment of the pair to one 8-side term.

Otherwise an entire pair is shared between one term of each row.
Their ratio (m-r)/(n-s) is b/c in lowest terms, where b divides 7!,
c divides 8!, 0<=r<7 and 0<=s<8. It satisfies b U_8 >= c L_7 since
1/7>1/8. Write n=s+ct, m=r+bt with t a positive integer. It solves

  7! (s+ct)_8 - 8! (r+bt)_7 = 0.

This polynomial is nonzero, of degree eight. Remove the coefficient
gcd and its initial power of t. By the rational root theorem, t
divides the resulting constant. Of the possible ratios 21 survive the
size test. Across all 1,176 equations c times the absolute constant
is at most 29,156,400, whereas n-s>=L_8=37,643,505,995,031,279.
This excludes the last case.

Run python Erdos849LocalInterpolation.py to check the interpolation
input and python Erdos849PairSevenEight.py for the comparisons and
all primitive polynomials.
