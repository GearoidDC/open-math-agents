The (3,5,7,8) common-value curve

Set f_k(X)=X(X-1)...(X-k+1)/k!. For K=(3,5,7,8), consider integer
points f_k(n_k)=a>=2, n_k>=2k for every k. Its affine curve over Q is
denoted by C, and its nonsingular projective completion by Cbar.

1. Localized restrictions on the prime divisors of a

For u=(u_k) with 0<=u_k<k, put H_u=gcd(a, (n_k-u_k) for k in K).
Write D=840, g=924. For every integer m>g there are positive, effectively
computable constants c_m,C_m, depending only on m and the displayed
columns, such that all admissible solutions satisfy

   c_m a^((1-g/m)/D) <= H_u <= C_m a^((1+g/m)/D)                  (1)

simultaneously for all 840 tuples. Thus, for any epsilon>0, one can
compute a threshold above which each log(H_u)/log(a) lies within
epsilon of 1/840. This is an effective restriction even though existence
of arbitrarily large points on a fixed curve is already ruled out by
qualitative finiteness theorems.

In particular, above an effectively computable absolute cutoff, each
individual factor n_k-s (0<=s<k) and a share at least D/k distinct
prime divisors. The counts per individual factor are

             column            3      5      7      8
             common primes   280    168    120    105.

For example, the admissible eighth-column rows for which any of the
eight consecutive integers n_8-7,...,n_8 has at most 104 prime divisors
are effectively bounded, regardless of the total prime count of a.
More generally, after fixing any subset of coordinates u_k=s_k, at
least the product of the remaining column indices counts distinct primes
shared by a and all the fixed factors. The proof supplies an algorithm
for the cutoff. A practical numerical cutoff has not been computed.

Geometry used in (1). The maps f_k are totally ramified at infinity.
For these four degrees the finite branch sets are disjoint, as verified
in section 2. Each small loop around a finite branch value therefore
acts on only one coordinate, and these loops generate each transitive
single-column monodromy group. The product is transitive, giving a
geometrically irreducible curve with degree D over the t-line, t=a.
On the affine chart at most one f'_k vanishes at once, so the equations
f_k(X_k)=t have full Jacobian rank and the coordinate ring R is smooth.
At infinity the simultaneous cycle has length lcm(3,5,7,8)=840,
giving a unique rational place I. Its orders of pole are D for t and
D/k for X_k. At t=0 lie the 840 distinct rational smooth points P_u
with coordinates u. The genus follows from section 2.

The divisibility argument. Let R=Q[t,(X_k)]/(f_k(X_k)-t for all k).
For e=v_p(a)>0, each column has an offset u_k for which p^e divides
n_k-u_k. Indeed, if v is the greatest valuation among its k factors,
then at each level h<=v there are at most floor(k/p^h)+1 multiples of
p^h in k consecutive integers; higher levels contribute none. Removing
one factor of valuation v leaves total valuation <=sum_h floor(k/p^h)
=v_p(k!). Since the entire product has valuation e+v_p(k!), v>=e.
Choosing one such offset in each column gives a full tuple w with
p^e | H_w.

If F in R has vanishing order at least m at P_w, then F is in the
m-th power of its maximal ideal in R, generated there by t and all
X_k-w_k. To see the global ideal membership, localize at the maximal
ideal. The local ring is a discrete valuation ring. The quotient by
the m-th power is already local Artinian, so localization preserves
that quotient. Clearing rational denominators in the resulting finite
sum of polynomial multiples of degree-m products, choose an integer
c>0 such that cF evaluated at an integral point is an integer multiple
of H_w^m. A single c works for any prescribed finite set of tuples.

By Riemann--Roch choose a nonzero rational function in

   L((m+g) I - m P_u).

It is regular on the smooth affine curve, hence lies in R. Any F in
L(N I) has magnitude O_F(a^(N/D)) on admissible integer points.
For an explicit reason, reduce its representative to monomials
t^h product X_k^i_k, 0<=i_k<k, h>=0, using the monic equations.
The pole orders hD+sum i_k(D/k) are distinct: reduction modulo each
k first recovers i_k because the degrees are pairwise coprime. Thus
each surviving monomial has weight <=N. Use n_k<=k-1+(k!a)^(1/k)
and the known coefficients to compute the constant. Off the finitely
many zeros of F we now have H_u^m<=|cF|<=O_F(a^((m+g)/D)).

For the other inequality choose a nonzero F in

   L(((D-1)m+g) I - m sum_{w != u} P_w).

Its defining divisor also has degree g. Consider any p^e exactly
dividing a. If v_p(H_u)<e, the full tuple w found above is different
from u. Consequently p^(me) divides cF at the solution. If v_p(H_u)
=e this prime is absent from a/H_u. Hence (a/H_u)^m divides cF.
Off its zeros this gives the lower bound in (1).

All computations used here are effective over Q. For instance enumerate
the bounded-weight monomials, solve the linear jet conditions at the
rational P_w using t as local parameter (all f'_k(w_k) are nonzero),
and clear denominators in ideal representations by linear algebra or
Groebner reduction. The norm of each selected F from Q(C) to Q(t)
is a nonzero polynomial in t, obtained as the determinant of
multiplication on the 840 reduced monomials over Q[t]. Thus one can
bound all positive integral a where F vanishes using an ordinary root
bound. A finite adjustment enlarges C_m and shrinks c_m to cover these
values and all tuples, since a>=2 and H_u>=1.

To count primes, take m=2g and choose a large enough cutoff that every
H_u>lcm(1,2,...,7)=420. Some prime p occurring in H_u then has a
power p^v>7 dividing H_u. No prime can serve two distinct tuples in
this way: at an offset where they differ, the difference is nonzero
with absolute value <=7 and would have to be divisible by a power >7.
Each tuple can therefore be assigned a different prime. Restricting
to the tuples containing a prescribed list of offsets proves the
localized prime counts above. The same argument gives (1) for other
fixed sets of pairwise coprime columns with disjoint finite branch
sets, with the corresponding D and g.

2. The reflection quotient and maps to small-genus curves

Modulo 13 the monic finite branch polynomials Res_X((X)_k-k!t, ((X)_k)')
up to units have the following coefficients, in ascending powers of t:

    3     10,0,1
    5     2,0,9,0,1
    7     6,0,10,0,5,0,1
    8     5,8,12,1,10,4,0,1.

They retain their degrees k-1 and all six pairwise gcds are 1 modulo
13. Thus the finite branch loci are pairwise disjoint in characteristic
zero. Erdos849SmallestCurve.py reproduces them by evaluating and
interpolating the resultants in the finite field. The repeated factors
for k=8 reflect the symmetry X->7-X and do not affect disjointness.

Passing to Y=(X_8-7/2)^2 gives a degree-four polynomial map in Y;
its branch values form a subset of the degree-eight finite branch
values. For any selection of degrees d_1,...,d_r among 3,5,7 and at
most one of 4 (the reflection quotient), 8, put d=product d_i. All are
pairwise coprime. Riemann--Hurwitz gives

    g=(d(r-1)-sum_i d/d_i+1)/2.

Indeed the ramification contribution from each polynomial at its finite
branch points is (d/d_i)(d_i-1), with the extra d-1 at infinity.
The full curve has genus 924; quotienting by just the degree-eight
reflection gives genus 436. The pairs of coordinates yield at least
genus 3, attained by the degree-3 and reflected degree-4 pair.

These are all intermediate fields between Qbar(t) and Qbar(C).
Over a common unbranched base the total monodromy is the product of
the single groups. For each odd degree p, the finite simple branch
transpositions generate S_p. For degree eight the group is
W=(C2)^4 semidirect S4: the quartic on blocks has distinct simple
critical values, and the critical value at X=7/2 gives a single
flip whose conjugates flip every block. These facts follow also from
the critical point calculation in Erdos849ReciprocalRunge.txt.
An intermediate field corresponds to an overgroup U of the product
of the four point stabilizers. In each odd factor it projects to
S_(p-1) or S_p. In the latter case U contains all S_p in that
coordinate, since it contains S_(p-1) there and its conjugates by U
generate S_p. In the former case the whole projected subgroup is
already present in that coordinate. Strip off these three direct
factors. In W, a subgroup containing the sheet stabilizer projects
on block permutations to S3 or S4. For S4 it contains all individual
flips by conjugating the flips of the other three blocks in the
stabilizer; the group is W. For S3 it lies in the block stabilizer,
of index two over the sheet stabilizer. This leaves only the full,
reflected and discarded eighth-column choices. Thus there are exactly
24 intermediate fields including both endpoints. None of the positive-
genus intermediate curves has genus 1 or 2.

An arbitrary morphism need not retain t. Nevertheless every nonconstant
geometric map from Cbar to genus zero has degree at least 87 (and at
most 105 for a minimal one, by projecting to X_8). For targets of
genus one or two the lower bounds are 69 and 57, respectively.

For the proof consider the tower dropping columns 3, then 5, leaving
the (7,8) curve. The genera in succession are 924, 215, 21. At a
step of prime degree k with lower curve of genus g', let b be the
degree of the present map to a target of genus h. Unless the map
factors through that step, their fields generate the entire upper
field. Castelnuovo--Severi gives

        b >= ceil((g-k g'+k-1)/(h+k-1)).

If it factors, its degree over the lower curve becomes b/k. On the
first step independence gives 141, 94, 71 for h=0,1,2. On the second
it gives 87,69,57 when converted to degree over the original curve.

The remaining (7,8) curve is a smooth plane octic: in its degree-eight
homogenization the only point at infinity is [1:0:0], and the
Z-derivative there is nonzero because the other polynomial has
degree seven. The affine chart is smooth by disjoint finite branch
loci. Adjunction identifies its canonical sections with the plane
quintics. Any divisor of at most six distinct points imposes
independent conditions on quintics, by taking for each point a
product of lines through the others and avoiding that point. By
Riemann--Roch it cannot move in a pencil. The octic thus has
gonality seven (projection from a point of it gives the upper bound).

It has no map of degree at most four to an elliptic curve. Degree
<=3 would give a map of degree <=6 to the line. For degree four,
fix two distinct generic fibers A,B of four points each. Their union
moves in a pencil, since two points on an elliptic curve move in a
pencil. Hence the eight points fail to impose independent conditions
on quintics, forcing at least seven collinear. Indeed, in the absence
of seven collinear, fix any one of the eight points x. Among the
other seven points find two disjoint pairs whose joining lines miss
x: grouped by direction from x, each class has size at most five.
If the largest class has size at least two, pair two of its elements
with two outside; if all are singletons any two pairs work. The
remaining three points can each be covered by a line avoiding x.
Their product is a separating quintic. Now at least three members
of A lie on the seven-point line. This determines a unique line for
the fixed A, since two triples in a four-set share two members. For
every generic B the same line contains at least three of its points,
contradicting the finite intersection of that line with the octic.

For a genus-two target the hyperelliptic double cover and gonality
seven imply degree at least four. Multiplying the octic's three lower
bounds 7,5,4 by 15 and taking the minima with the previous two steps
gives 87,69,57. These inequalities describe a barrier to low-degree
quotient methods. They do not classify the integer points of the octic
or of the genus-924 curve.

3. A height-independent class of excluded values

Gyory, Theorem 2, gives the full classification for x^l=binom(n,k),
x,l>1, k>=2, n>=2k. The possibilities are k=l=2, or the single
solution (n,k,x,l)=(50,3,140,2). Thus a perfect power a>=2 has
half-row multiplicity exactly 2 if it is triangular and square or if
a=19600. All other perfect powers have half-row multiplicity 1.
Indeed 395^2<8*19600+1<396^2, and so its exceptional row cannot
coexist with a triangular row. All values also have the row (a,1).

A stronger uniform obstruction allows unrestricted factors at small
primes. If k>=4 is any admissible column, then

     gcd { v_p(a) : p>k, p|a } = 1.                                (2)

Sylvester's theorem gives a prime p>k in the interval product k!a
since its first term n_k-k+1 exceeds k. This prime divides a, so
the gcd is over a nonempty set. If some prime exponent l divided
all the listed valuations, the canonical l-th-power-free part b of
k!a would be supported on primes <=k. Write k!a=b x^l. The chosen
prime p>k divides x. Theorem 2 of Saradha and Gyory in Gyory's 1998
paper excludes every such length-k interval product for k>=4.
This proves (2). In particular one can discard the complete factors
of a at 2,3,5,7 before testing the perfect-power obstruction for the
(3,5,7,8) pattern. If the remainder is one or is a perfect power,
no such four-column representation exists at any height. This test
also applies to any a known to occupy a proper column at least 8:
if every valuation at p>7 were divisible by l, the same would hold
in the nonempty subset of primes p>k, contradicting (2).

This also gives two exact unrestricted multiplicity conclusions.

* Suppose a=2^A 3^B y^l for nonnegative A,B and integers y>=1,l>=2.
  Then M(a)=2 if a is triangular with a>=6 or is 19600, and M(a)=1
  otherwise.
  Indeed (2) excludes k>=4. For k=3 the identical interval-product
  argument applies with the single exception (48,3,6,140,2) in
  Gyory's 1998 Theorem 2. It yields a=19600. The column k=2 gives
  exactly the triangular values in this class with index n>=4.

* Suppose a=2^A 3^B 5^E y^l with A,B,E>=0 and y>=1,l>=2. Then
  M(a)<=2 except for M(120)=3. Indeed (2) forbids every k>=5.
  The solved pairs among 2,3,4 give, after imposing n>=2k,
  the only double proper values 120,1540,7140 for (2,3), 210 for
  (2,4), and none for (3,4). Among these only 120 belongs to the
  stated class; after stripping 2,3,5 each other value has a prime
  of exponent one. The three rows for 120 are 120,16,10.
