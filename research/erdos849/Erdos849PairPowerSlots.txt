Twenty-four distinct primes in a common value of columns seven and eight

If a=C(n,8)=C(m,7)>=X=10^128 with n>=16 and m>=14, every one of
n-7,...,n hosts at least three distinct full prime powers of a when
each full power is assigned to its term of maximal valuation. In
particular a has at least 24 distinct prime divisors.

Put Y=a/X. For each pair of offsets 0<=s<8, 0<=r<7 the accompanying
archive supplies an integral polynomial F in the shifted rows and a
vanishing order h. The local divisibility and real nonvanishing checks
are exactly those in Erdos849InterpolatedPowers.txt. Here 19W<=56h for
every polynomial, so the ordinary prime powers at slot s are bounded
by Q_s Y^(1/19). For p=2,3,5,7 the respective bounds are
Q_{s,p} Y^(1/19). The constants are maximized over r. The verifier
checks three candidate exponent thresholds for each prime to sharpen
the Taylor denominator estimates and takes the smallest valid bound.
All Q_s exceed 8, and their maximum is 3,967,273.

Assign each full prime power to a term of maximal valuation. The
cofactor b_s of n-s divides M=lcm(1,...,8)=840, by the elementary
valuation argument in section 1 of Erdos849NineteenLow.txt.
Call a power exceptional only if it exceeds its ordinary bound, so its
base is one of 2,3,5,7 and the power exceeds 8. For such a base, write
e=v_p(a) and V=v_p(n-s). Every other term n-v in the row has
v_p(n-v)=v_p(s-v) because p^e>8 as an integer and v_p(n-s)>=e.
Consequently

  v_p(b_s)=V-e=v_p(8!)-v_p(s! (7-s)!)=v_p(8*C(7,s)).

For each possible collection of at most two hosted powers the checker
starts with M and replaces its p-part by the p-part of 8*C(7,s) for
each exceptional base in the collection. This gives a cofactor bound B.
Let q_i,q_j be the corresponding bounds Q_s or Q_{s,p}. Use ordinary
unit factors to pad an empty or singleton slot to two factors. The
checker verifies the strict inequality

  B q_i q_j < floor((8!X)^(1/8))-s

in every case, allowing two ordinary factors and requiring different
exceptional bases. Two hosted factors would imply

  n-s <= B q_i q_j Y^(2/19)
       < (floor((8!X)^(1/8))-s) Y^(1/8)
       <= n-s,

since 2/19<1/8. Thus at least three powers are assigned to each slot.
Run python Erdos849PairPowerSlots.py to recheck all 56 polynomials
and the final inequalities with integer arithmetic.
