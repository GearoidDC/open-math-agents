Twenty distinct primes when the largest of four proper columns is at most 17

Suppose a>=X=10^128 occurs in column 1 and at least four distinct
proper columns from 2,...,17. Then a has at least 20 distinct prime
divisors. The same argument only needs the four proper columns.

1. First reduction

Suppose there are at most 19 prime bases. Use the host-assignment lemma
and allocation test of Erdos849NineteenLow.txt with a prime-factor cap
of nineteen. Take nonnegative occupancies of the k numerator
terms summing to 19. Pad by units if fewer actual powers occur. Every
cofactor divides M_k=lcm(1,...,k), and their product is k!.

The interpolation archive supplies for each available pair
7<=j<d<=17 bounds P<=Q_p (a/X)^(1/17), where one constant covers
ordinary primes and one covers each exceptional small prime base.
Take the componentwise minimum over all available pairs in the pattern.
For the ordinary constant Q and a slot containing t factors, the
cofactor is at least
  max(1,(floor((k!X)^(1/k))-k+1)/Q^t)
up to the losses of its exceptional bases, provided tk<=17. If tk>17
use the trivial bound 1. Each small-prime loss can be applied in at most
one slot. The checker enumerates every unordered allocation and compares
with the individual cofactor bound M_k and the full product k!.

The nine published fixed-column pairs and the (7,8) exclusion of
Erdos849PairPowerSlots.txt are imposed first. For every surviving
pattern at least one certified pair is available. The allocation test
using only the original Erdos849InterpolatedPowers.json.gz archive leaves
the following eight choices:
  (3,5,8,17), (5,6,8,17),
  (i,5,9,d) as unordered sets, with i in {3,4,6}, d in {16,17}.
Each includes column 5 and one of (8,17),(9,16),(9,17).

2. A bound strong enough for column five

Put Y=a/X and T=120013011. For all 433 offset pairs of the three
remaining column pairs we show
  P<=T Y^(1/15) for each full prime power P=p^e||a.
Use the original branch polynomial except for four polynomials of
orders 6 or 7 stored in Erdos849TwentyLow.json. The checker verifies
the exact Taylor conditions, the uniform nonvanishing, and the
archimedean bound |F|<=H Y^(w/(jd)) as in
Erdos849InterpolatedPowers.txt. In every case 15w<jdh and H<T^h.
For p not dividing the local series denominator L, this directly
implies the claim, since P^h divides the nonzero value F. Trivial
small powers P<=d already satisfy the bound.

For p dividing L, let f=v_p(L) and e0 be the least integer with
p^e0>T. A possible exception has e>=e0. Compute
N=ceil(h e0/(e0-f)) and the integral coefficients c_t of the local
series F mod z^N, where z=a/L. The terms of order below h vanish.
As in the earlier Taylor refinement,
  delta=max(0,max_{h<=t<N,c_t!=0}(h e0-t(e0-f)-v_p(c_t)))
gives p^(he) dividing p^delta F. The tail of degree at least N has
valuation at least h e. Hence
  P^h<=p^delta H Y^(w/(jd)).
If contrary to the claim P>T Y^(1/15), substitute Y<(P/T)^15.
Since jdh-15w>0 and P>=p^e0, this implies strictly
  p^(e0*(jdh-15w)) T^(15w) < (p^delta H)^(jd).
The checker verifies the opposite weak inequality on every branch.

Finally 60 T^3<floor((5!X)^(1/5))-4. Any of the five numerator terms
assigned at most three factors would be at most
  lcm(1,...,5) T^3 Y^(3/15)=60 T^3 Y^(1/5),
strictly below its lower bound. Each of the five terms therefore
requires at least four factors, contradicting the nineteen-prime cap.

Run python Erdos849TwentyLow.py to reproduce both reductions. No
intermediate lattice-search files or probabilistic computations are used.
