Six prime divisors above 100 in two ranges for five-row values

Every five-row value a up to C(2^64-1,8), and every such value at or
above 10^3133, has at least six distinct prime divisors greater than
100. If a five-row value has only five such divisors, four proper
columns must be either {3,5,7,8} or {5,6,7,8}; its value lies strictly
between the two bounds. In its column-eight row, exactly three
numerator factors are 100-smooth, with offsets {0,3,6} or {1,4,7}.
The finite search through 10^128 is in Erdos849Focused128.md. The
result for largest column at least nine is in Erdos849SmoothNeighbors.md.

1. A finite calculation in a single column

Suppose 10^128 <= a=C(n,8) and n<=2^64-1. Then a has at least six
distinct prime divisors above 100. This applies to every value in this
column. The upper endpoint C(2^64-1,8) is about 3.32535 * 10^149.

If there are at most five such primes, at least three of the eight
numerator terms are 100-smooth. A prime above 100 occurs in at most
one of those terms and survives division by 8!. Among any three terms
there are two at distance at most three. All terms lie between
floor((8! 10^128)^(1/8))-7 = 37643505995031279 and 2^64-1.

The corrected Luca--Najman Theorem 1 gives precisely 16,223 centers
x>=2 at which x-1 and x+1 are both 100-smooth. The checker verifies
all stored centers and the cardinality. This gives all pairs of gap
two directly and all pairs of gap one by dividing an even pair by two.
A gap-three pair of multiples of three is three times an adjacent pair.
These cases give eight pairs in our interval and 48 compatible end
rows. None of the rows has three smooth terms.
Source: https://web.math.pmf.unizg.hr/~fnajman/FLFNMC.pdf

For any other smooth gap-three pair, one endpoint is odd and coprime
to three. Erdos849EightSmooth.c enumerates products of primes 5 to 97
in sorted order, including repetitions. Division of the upper endpoint
by the next factor prevents integer overflow before multiplication.
The extension by a prime is taken only when the new product is within
the upper limit. Every eligible anchor is reached once by unique
factorization. There are exactly 1,431,208,549 anchors in the interval.
Neither neighbor at distance three was smooth and within the interval
for any of those anchors.

An independent count partitions the 23 primes into alternating
subsets. They give 4,316,984 and 1,719,142 products up to 2^64-1,
respectively, including 1. For each product in the first list, count
the second-list multipliers bringing it into the stated interval
by binary search. The total is again 1,431,208,549.

Run python -B Erdos849EightSmooth.py. It needs a C compiler and accepts
--cc PATH. The Python wrapper cross-checks the C enumeration by direct
enumeration on [100,10000] before scanning the full interval.

2. A necessary condition at every height above 10^128

A row with at most five primes above 100 must contain exactly three
smooth terms in arithmetic progression with common difference three.
All three are coprime to three. In particular its smooth terms have
offsets {0,3,6} or {1,4,7} among the eight numerator factors.

Indeed an odd center x of a smooth pair x-2,x+2 is at most
407479035814853 by Filip Najman's Theorem 3(a), below our lower bound.
An even center of such a pair reduces by division by two to a smooth
gap-two pair. Without any upper bound on n, the complete known lists
for gaps one and two, doubled gap-two pairs, and tripled gap-one pairs
leave 15 pairs above the lower bound. Their 79 compatible end rows
have at most two smooth factors. Thus no pair at gap one, two or four,
or pair of multiples of three at gap three, can occur with a third
smooth term. Among three positions spanning at most seven with these
excluded gaps, both successive gaps must equal three. A fourth
position would force a gap of at most two.
Source: https://web.math.pmf.unizg.hr/~fnajman/smooth.pdf

3. A smaller tail for common columns 5, 7 and 8

If a>=10^3133 occurs in columns 5, 7 and 8, it has at least 31
distinct prime divisors. In particular at least six exceed 100. The
two surviving five-row patterns with largest column eight above 10^128
both contain these columns by the classifications of Blokhuis, Brouwer
and de Weger, Theorem 2.2. For largest column at least nine the
separate smooth-neighbor calculation already gives six primes above
100 at all heights relevant here.
Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/1707.06893

Let H=10^3133 and write n_k for the row in column k. For a full
prime power p^e in a, put s_k = n_k mod p^e if that residue is <k.
This is always so: the factorial floor formula supplies a level
i>=e at which n_k mod p^i < k mod p^i <= k. Write x_k=n_k-s_k,
c_k=k!/product_(v!=s_k)(s_k-v) with 0<=v<k. Let D=420 and let
L be the least common multiple of denominators of c_k/(s_k-v)
over these three rows. Then L|D. For e>v_p(D), all x_k/(s_k-v)
have positive valuation. The equation for u_k=x_k/(c_k L) is

    u_k product_(v!=s_k)(1 + u_k c_k L/(s_k-v)) = z,   z=a/L.

The linear coefficient in u_k is one and the other coefficients are
integral. In the p-adic integers u_k is the integral power series in z
obtained by formal inversion at zero. Indeed the binomial product
formula gives v_p(u_k)=v_p(z)>0, and the divided difference of the
left side at any two p-divisible arguments is a unit. Reducing the
formal inverse modulo z^h therefore gives the actual solution modulo
p^(h(e-v_p(L))).

Erdos849EightSmoothTail.py constructs a polynomial F in the three
shifted rows for every one of 280 offset triples. Integer elimination
on the lowest 24 nonconstant monomials of distinct weights gives the
polynomials. Exponents 0<=i_k<k have integer weight sum i_k*(280/k).
The checker recomputes the substituted Taylor coefficients and checks
that coefficients of degrees 0,...,h-1 vanish. Thus p^(h(e-v_p(L)))
divides the value of F when e>v_p(D).

Put t=(a/H)^(1/280), R_k=floor((k! H)^(1/k)). The binomial product
bounds give (R_k-s_k)t^(280/k) <= x_k <= (R_k+k-s_k)t^(280/k).
At t=1 the lower bound on the absolute value of the leading monomial
exceeds the sum of upper bounds on all opposite-sign terms. Since
the leading monomial has greatest weight W, this proves F!=0 for
every t>=1. The checker also bounds |F| <= B*t^W, taking B to be
the larger sign-separated sum of upper bounds at t=1. It verifies

    30W <= 280h,   B < Q^h,   Q=floor(((H-1)/420)^(1/30)).

In the displayed definition the integer floor in the radicand can be
taken first. Consequently p^e < p^v_p(D) * (a/D)^(1/30) for every
p|a, including e<=v_p(D) by a>D. If at most 30 primes divide a,
multiplying gives a < D*(a/D)^(omega(a)/30) <= a, a contradiction.
Every branch is checked exactly with integer arithmetic and h<=24.
Replay with python -B Erdos849EightSmoothTail.py. This leaves the
interval C(2^64-1,8)<a<10^3133 open for the six-prime question in
five-row patterns with largest column eight.

4. An unconditional effective tail using only column eight

If two numerator factors B>A are 100-smooth, then g=B-A<=7 and
B/g + (-A/g) = 1 is a rational S-unit equation with S the 25 primes
under 100. In Corollary 7.2, Rafael von Kanel gives the bound
h(B/g)<=3 n_S(log n_S)^2/2+65, where n_S=128*product_(p in S) p.
Here n_S<3*10^38 and log n_S<90, so the height is below 4*10^42.
The rational height implies B<=7 exp(4*10^42), hence
n<=B+7 and a<=n^8<10^(10^44). Thus above the latter cutoff every
column-eight value has at least seven distinct primes above 100.
Source: https://doi.org/10.1112/TLMS/TLU003
