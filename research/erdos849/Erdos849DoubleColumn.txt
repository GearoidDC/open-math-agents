The (7,14) boundary under a fourteen-prime cap

Let a = C(n,14) = C(m,7), with 28 <= n and 14 <= m, and suppose a has at
most fourteen distinct prime factors. We prove analytically that

    n <= 14! + 13,       a <= C(14!+13,14) < 10^143.

In fact every possible n >= 10^6 has an empty numerator position in the
fourteenth column. The accompanying elementary finite check eliminates all
remaining n, giving an all-size exclusion for this pair of columns.

1. Prime powers in the two rows

For each prime p dividing a, take its complete power P=p^v_p(a). In a
representation a=C(u,k), assign P to offset h=u mod P. Then h<k and
P divides u-h. One proof of h<k uses Kummer's count of carries in adding
k and u-k. At least v_p(a) numbered levels carry, so there is a carrying
level i >= v_p(a). At such a level u mod p^i < k mod p^i <= k; hence
u mod P <= u mod p^i < k as well. This statement is formalized as
Erdos849PrimeHosts.residue_lt_column.

The powers at any one offset have coprime bases, so their product divides
that numerator term. Write the term as this product times an integral
cofactor. The products of the terms and of the assigned prime powers are
k!a and a, respectively. Consequently the k cofactors multiply to k!.
These statements are formalized in Erdos849HostCofactors. An empty offset
has no assigned prime power, so u-h itself divides k!.

2. Two factors per position in the seventh column

Assume n >= 10^6 and that no fourteenth-column position is empty. The cap
forces exactly fourteen primes, each assigned to a different position.
Write n-s=b_s P_s, s=0,...,13, with P_s the complete power at s. Then
the b_s are positive integers and their product is 14!. Assign the same
fourteen powers to the seven positions m-r in the other row. This gives
a partition of the fourteen offsets into seven groups G_r, with

    m-r = c_r * product_{s in G_r} P_s,       product_r c_r = 7!.

Put D=14!/7!=17297280 and theta=D^(-1/7). Comparing the two falling
factorials gives

    theta*(n-13)^2 <= m <= theta*n^2 + 6.

Indeed, (m)_7=(n)_14/D, with subscripts denoting falling factorials.
The bounds follow by bounding each of the seven or fourteen factors
between the smallest and the largest individual term. Integer seventh
powers give 92/1000 < theta < 93/1000; in particular theta>1/16.

If any group G_r had at most one member, P_s<=n and c_r<=7! would yield
m<=7!n+6, also in the empty case. But n-13>n/2, so the displayed lower
bound gives m>n^2/64>7!n+6 for n>=10^6. Each of the seven groups must
therefore have precisely two members.

3. A forbidden small denominator

For a pair G_r={s,t}, let B=b_s b_t and R=c_r/B. Its row equation gives

    m-r = R*(n-s)*(n-t).

The seven positive denominators B multiply to 14!. One of them satisfies
B<=36, since 37^7=94931877133 > 14!=87178291200. Use that pair.

For clarity, we give an explicit uniform error bound. Since s,t<=13 and
0<=r<=6, the preceding upper bound on m, theta<93/1000, and n>=10^6
imply 0<R<1: the numerator is at most theta*n^2+6<n^2/4 and the
denominator is at least (n-13)^2>n^2/4. Also

    theta - m/n^2 <= theta*(26/n - 169/n^2) < 3/n,
    m/n^2 - theta <= 6/n^2 < 3/n.

From m/n^2 = R*(1-s/n)*(1-t/n) + r/n^2 it follows that

    |R - m/n^2| <= max(R*(s+t)/n, 6/n^2) <= 26/n.

Thus |R-theta|<29/n. In particular,

    1/11 < 92/1000 - 29/10^6 < R < 93/1000 + 29/10^6 < 3/32.

No fraction of denominator at most 36 lies in this interval. Indeed, if
1/11 < u/v < 3/32, both 11u-v and 3v-32u are positive integers, and

    v = 32*(11u-v) + 11*(3v-32u) >= 43.

Taking u=c_r and v=B contradicts B<=36, even if the fraction has common
factors. This proves the claimed empty position for n>=10^6. The proof
uses a rational gap and does not need an irrationality-measure estimate.

4. Height and finite exclusion

An empty term n-s divides 14!, whence n<=14!+13. The conclusion also
holds for n<10^6. Monotonicity in the row gives the stated bound on a.
For a>=10^100 we necessarily have n>=10^6 (since C(n,14)<=n^14).
Therefore every candidate in this range already belongs to the empty
fourteenth-column list, at the exact edge j=7.

The standalone check is run with

    python -B Erdos849DoubleColumn.py

It verifies the numerical inequalities with integers and rational numbers.
For 28<=n<10^6 it tests C(n,14) against the seventh-column residues modulo
primes between 71 and 499. Each modulus exceeds 14, so the coefficients
are polynomials of their row residues in the prime field. All 999972 rows
are excluded; the script contains an exact inverse fallback for survivors.
For n>=10^6 the script forms the full set of n=b+s with b a positive
divisor of 14! and 0<=s<14. The 2592 divisors give 14028 distinct larger
rows. Direct exact inversion of column seven excludes all 14028. Of these
larger rows, 3094 have coefficient at least 10^100, agreeing with the
separate Erdos849FourteenAudit.py run. No fixed-column literature result
or large-interval sieve is needed for this standalone boundary argument.
