Twenty-one prime divisors when four proper columns are at most seventeen

Let a>=X=10^128 occur in four distinct proper columns at most 17,
with n>=2k for a representation C(n,k)=a. Then a has at least
21 distinct prime divisors.

1. Enumeration of column patterns

Suppose there are at most twenty prime bases. The first reduction
repeats Erdos849TwentyLow.txt with a cap of twenty. Four proper
columns avoiding the published pairs always have two columns 7<=j<d.
The original polynomial archive gives P<=Q_p Y^(1/17) for every full
prime power P=p^v_p(a), Y=a/X. Ordinary primes share one constant,
and 2,3,5,7,11,13 each have a separate constant. Choose the smallest
available bound separately for each prime-base category among the
occupied column pairs.

In a row of column k assign every power to a numerator term of maximal
p-adic valuation. Every term divided by its assigned powers is a
positive integer cofactor dividing M_k=lcm(1,...,k); the product of
these cofactors is k! (Erdos849NineteenLow.txt, section 1). Enumerate
all unordered k-tuples of nonnegative occupancies summing to twenty,
padding by ordinary units if necessary. The lower bounds for
individual cofactors and their complete product from the previous
calculation exclude every tuple except for the 62 column patterns
identified by the verifier. This step uses the nine classified
pairs and the separate (7,8) result from Erdos849PairPowerSlots.txt.
Every surviving pattern contains at least one of the following pairs:

  (7,9), (7,10), (8,9), (8,10), (9,10),
  (j,d) with j in {8,9,10,11} and d in {16,17}.

2. Local polynomials for thirteen pairs

For each remaining pair choose a target column k, either 7, 8, 9, 16
or 17, as listed in the verifier. Put t=floor(20/k). For every offset
pair, an integral polynomial with vanishing order h, largest weight w
and absolute-value bound H is verified exactly as in
Erdos849InterpolatedPowers.txt. The original certificate is used unless
the supplementary archive gives a replacement. Check ktw<=jdh and set
  Q = max over offset pairs of ceil(H^(1/h)).
Writing D=lcm(1,...,d-1), the local divisibility estimate gives
  P <= p^v_p(D) Q Y^(1/(kt)).
Indeed the local denominator divides D, p^(h(e-v_p(D))) divides
the nonzero integer F, and |F|<=H Y^(w/(jd)). When P<=d the bound
holds directly. Exactly the same bound applies no matter which
k-column slot hosts the prime power.

3. Cofactor contradiction in the target column

Let L=floor((k!X)^(1/k))-k+1, so every term is at least L Y^(1/k).
Write A_u for the product of the u largest full prime powers dividing
D, using all if u exceeds their number, and A_0=1. At most u distinct
small bases can contribute an exceptional loss to u factors in a slot.
The verifier checks
  L > M_k A_(t-1) Q^(t-1).
Thus a slot with at most t-1 powers is smaller than its lower bound;
padding by units handles fewer than t-1. Every slot has at least t
powers.

For k=7, t=2 and the stronger comparison L>M_k A_2 Q^2 holds, so
all seven slots have at least three powers, immediately giving 21.
Otherwise at least b=(t+1)k-20 slots have exactly t powers. Select
any b such slots. Each cofactor is at least L/Q^t divided by the
exceptional factors p^v_p(D) in its assigned powers. These exceptional
factors have product at most D across all b slots, since each prime
base is assigned once in a row. Consequently
  k! >= (L/Q^t)^b / D.
The verifier checks the opposite strict inequality using integers.
This disposes of all thirteen pairs, hence all 62 initial patterns.

Run python Erdos849TwentyOneLow.py with the supplementary
Erdos849TwentyOneLow.json.gz and the existing interpolation files.
Across the thirteen target pairs the checker verifies 1,629 local
branches. The supplementary archive stores 1,447 replacements and
uses the original data for the other 182 branches.
