Four simultaneous columns and the large prime divisors of their common value

Let a=C(n_3,3)=C(n_5,5)=C(n_7,7)=C(n_8,8), with admissible rows.
The checker and its certificate file use the column order (8,7,5,3).
For a>=10^128 it proves at least 31 distinct prime divisors. A second
argument using the 25 primes below 100 proves that at least seven
divisors exceed 100. A finite congruence check strengthens seven to
ten. Here K=128, Q=34800, and R=36.

Local prime powers

Write H=10^K. The certificate parameters K, Q and R specify the uniform
bound

    p^e <= p^v_p(420) Q (a/H)^(1/R),   p^e || a, a >= H.          (1)

There are 840 offset tuples s with 0<=s_k<k. For any p^e || a the
factorial floor formula gives such an s with p^e dividing n_k-s_k
in every row. Indeed there are e distinct levels with a carry, so
some level i>=e has n_k mod p^i < k mod p^i <= k. Put x_k=n_k-s_k,
c_k=k!/product_(0<=v<k,v!=s_k)(s_k-v), and let L be the common
denominator of all the rational numbers c_k/(s_k-v). Then L|420.

If e>v_p(420), each x_k/(s_k-v) is p-divisible. The row equation is

    u_k product_(v!=s_k)(1+c_k L u_k/(s_k-v))=a/L,    u_k=x_k/(c_k L).

The product equation for x_k shows v_p(u_k)=e-v_p(L)>0. The integral
formal inverse at zero exists because the linear coefficient in u_k is
one. Substituting z=a/L in its truncated series therefore gives the
actual u_k modulo p^(h(e-v_p(L))) through order h-1: the polynomial's
divided difference at two p-divisible arguments is a p-adic unit.

For each tuple the archive supplies an integer polynomial F(x_8,x_7,
x_5,x_3) and an order h. The checker substitutes the four integral
series to confirm that all coefficients through order h-1 vanish.
Hence p^(h(e-v_p(L))) divides F. To control its ordinary value, put
t=(a/H)^(1/840), z_k=floor((k!H)^(1/k)). The binomial product gives

    (z_k-s_k)t^(840/k) <= x_k <= (z_k+k-s_k)t^(840/k).

The weight of a monomial with exponents i_k is w=sum i_k*(840/k).
Orient the leading coefficient positively. For a lower bound replace
positive terms by their lower endpoints, negative terms by their
upper endpoints, retaining all powers t^w. In decreasing weight order
all partial sums of these signed coefficients are positive. Summation
by parts proves positivity for t>=1. Thus F at the row is nonzero.
The larger sum of the positive or negative upper absolute endpoints
is B. With W the greatest weight, |F|<=B t^W. The checker verifies
B<Q^h and R W<=840h. Together these give (1). When e<=v_p(420),
(1) holds already since Q>1. All certificate checks use integers.

Small prime powers

If at most m primes exceed 100, then there are at most 25+m prime
factors in total. Set Y=Q(a/H)^(1/R). A prime p<=100 contributes at
most p^v_p(420) times the largest integer power of p not exceeding Y.
Each of the other at most m primes contributes at most Y. Their
product therefore satisfies

    a <= 420 Y^m product_(p<=100) p^floor(log_p Y).

The checker uses integer powers to bound this expression on intervals
[10^(i/10),10^((i+1)/10)]. For the right endpoint use
1+floor((Q^(10R) 10^(i+1-10K))^(1/(10R))) in place of Y, then
compare the tenth power of the product with 10^i. Above the final
interval the simpler expression 420 Y^(25+m) suffices since R>25+m.
For m=6 this works on every tenth-decade up to 10^239.

For m=7 the continuous bound starts at 10^308. There are 63 earlier
tenth-decades in which the elementary product bound is insufficient.
On each of them, write U for the upper bound on the product and V for
the lower bound on a. Among the primes p>=31 up to 100 at most one
can miss its maximum exponent, since U<31^2 V. For seven such primes
having the largest maximal powers, at least six powers therefore
divide a. For each of the possible choices, n_8 modulo each required
prime power must belong to {0,...,7}. The checker enumerates these
residues by the Chinese remainder theorem, with the binomial product
inequalities providing the lower and upper bounds on n_8. Six merged
ranges give one candidate row. Direct exact binomial tests exclude it.
For m=8 the same argument allows more missed powers, as calculated
from the ratio U/V in each band. The 770 exceptional tenth-decades
merge into 72 ranges, leaving 69 candidate rows and zero common
values. Replay with python -B Erdos849PatternThreeCRT.py --large 8.
For m=9 the 2,013 exceptional tenth-decades merge into 181 ranges.
The Chinese remainder search leaves 4,378 eighth-column rows, none
of whose values is in all three other columns. The default replay
uses a two-wheel interval join. For coprime wheel moduli M,N with
residues b,c, a row is b+Mt with t congruent to (c-b)/M modulo N.
Sorting the residues c/M modulo N lets binary search select only
the entries that meet the required interval for t.
Run python -B Erdos849PatternThreeCRT.py.

Counting numerator factors

Assign each full power p^e || a to a column-eight numerator term of
largest p-adic valuation, choosing the first in case of a tie. It can
absorb the full power: the sum of valuations of the other seven
terms is at most v_p(8!) by counting at most floor(8/p^j) occurrences
at each level after the largest is removed. Dividing each term by its
assigned powers leaves a cofactor b_s, with product_s b_s=8!.

For a subset S of one or two of the eight positions, the upper bound
for the product of its terms given by (1) is C(S) Y^j, where j is the
number of powers assigned to S. To compute C(S) we bound the product
of its b_s and the assigned factors p^v_p(420). Only 2,3,5,7 matter.
For each such p, enumerate n modulo the first p^u>8. A nonzero residue
of n-s has known valuation. A zero residue is unique among the eight
terms and its valuation can be replaced by u: increasing it increases
the assigned exponent by the same amount, leaving b_s unchanged. The
checker permits the denominator loss at this position even if the
minimal exponent e is zero. It maximizes separately at each prime,
so no compatibility of the maximizing residues is assumed.

This gives C(S)<=117600 at one position and C(S)<=705600 at two.
If there are at most 30 powers, one position receives at most two,
or two positions each receive at most three. With M=z_8-7, both cases
are impossible if M>117600 Q^2 and M^2>705600 Q^6 at H, since every
term is at least M(a/H)^(1/8) and R>=24. This proves at least 31
distinct prime factors under those checked inequalities.

Reproduction

Run python -B Erdos849PatternThree.py. The compressed JSON file stores
the four offsets, the order, and each monomial's signed coefficient,
integer weight, and four exponents. The checker recomputes weights,
binomial endpoints, local divisibility, sign conditions, denominator
losses, finite prime products, and all 840 offset tuples. Discovery
uses recursive formal equation inversion. Replay independently uses
Lagrange inversion: for z=u phi(u), the coefficient of z^m in u^e
equals (e/m) times the coefficient of u^(m-e) in phi(u)^(-m).
The software used to discover the polynomials is unnecessary for
verification.
