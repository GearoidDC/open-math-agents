The binomial columns 3 and 5

Let C be the smooth projective curve over Q obtained by normalizing
C(n,3)=C(m,5). Its Jacobian is isogenous over Q to a product of two
nonisogenous Q-simple abelian surfaces. Their rational Mordell-Weil
ranks are at least 4 and 6 respectively, hence the full rank is at
least 10. Each surface has geometric Néron-Severi rank at most 2.
In particular the genus-four curve fails the usual sufficient rank
tests for both classical and quadratic Chabauty. Its positive-dimensional
Q-isogeny quotients fail the corresponding numerical tests as well.

1. Hyperelliptic factors

In centered coordinates X=n-1, Y=m-2 the equation is

  20(X^3-X)=Y(Y^2-1)(Y^2-4).

Write r=X/Y, A=5+20r^3, B=4+20r. Substitution gives Y^4-AY^2+B=0.
The involution (X,Y) -> (-X,-Y) fixes r, and its quotient C0 has equation

  v^2=F0(r)=400r^6+200r^3-80r+9,       v=2Y^2-A.

The two points at infinity of C0 are rational, with v/A approaching
+1 and -1. The binomial curve arises by taking a square root of
s=(A+v)/2. At the positive point at infinity s has a pole of order 3.
At the negative point it has a zero of order 2, since s(A-s)=B.
Its only finite zero lies at r=-1/5, v=-A, and has order 1 since
B has a simple zero and A(-1/5)=121/25 is nonzero. These are the
only branches of odd valuation. The sextic F0 is separable by good
reduction at 7. Riemann-Hurwitz now gives genus 4 for C. The point
at infinity of C maps to the positive infinity on C0, which is not
a Weierstrass point since the hyperelliptic coordinate r is unramified
there. In an admissible binomial representation Y=m-2 is positive,
so the quotient coordinate r=X/Y is defined.

On the further cover z^2=B, form u=20(Y+z/Y). Direct substitution
gives a second genus-two curve C1,

  u^2=F1(z)=(z^2-4)^3+800z+2000.

The field extension adjoining z to Q(C) has degree two: if B were
a square in Q(C0)(sqrt(s)), either B or B/s=A-s would be a square
in Q(C0). Each has a simple zero at r=-1/5, v=+A. The map to C1
and the two-to-one map to C define a correspondence. Pullback followed
by trace on differentials sends dz/u and z dz/u to

  -Y dr/(Y^4-B)    and    Y^3 dr/(Y^4-B).

Indeed dz/dr=10/z and tracing sums the values for z and -z. These
forms are independent and odd under simultaneous sign. The two
forms pulled back from C0 are independent and even. They give all
four regular differentials of C. Thus the morphism of Jacobians
to J(C0) x J(C1) obtained from the quotient and the correspondence
is an isogeny over Q.

2. Mordell-Weil lower bounds

Run python Erdos849ThreeFiveCurve.py to reproduce the arithmetic.
Both sextics are irreducible modulo 7. The checker tests their gcd
with x^(7^m)-x for m=1,2,3, exhausting all possible proper factors
of a reducible sextic. Over Q, the rational 2-torsion on each
Jacobian is therefore zero: a nonzero 2-torsion divisor on a genus-two
hyperelliptic curve corresponds to a two-element subset of the six
Weierstrass points, and transitivity forbids an invariant such subset.

For two distinct roots i,j of a sextic modulo an odd good prime p,
the divisor of (x-i)/(x-j) is twice the difference of the two
Weierstrass points. Evaluation on a degree-zero divisor gives a
homomorphism from the reduced Jacobian modulo twice itself to
F_p^*/(F_p^*)^2. This follows from Weil reciprocity, using disjoint
divisor representatives. Compose with good reduction of the rational
Jacobian. For points with x=a/b and base point x=a0/b0 the evaluation
is the square class of

  (a-i b)(a0-j b0) / ((a-j b)(a0-i b0)).

The checker verifies all stated rational point equations and displays
four independent binary rows on C0 and six on C1. On C0, for example,
the base point has coordinates (0,3). The four x-coordinates are
-1,1,-1/2,2/3. The square-class vectors read

  p=11,  i=2,  j=3       1000
  p=61,  i=15, j=53      0101
  p=73,  i=9,  j=69      1111
  p=83,  i=6,  j=7       0110.

On C1 the assigned base point has x=-8. On the nine other points,
in the order printed in the script's POINTS1 table, the six rows are

  p=17,  i=8,  j=16      011111001
  p=97,  i=14, j=60      110111011
  p=109, i=76, j=78      100101111
  p=139, i=75, j=130     011100010
  p=173, i=31, j=137     000011111
  p=191, i=67, j=178     100101100.

Linear independence bounds the dimensions of J(Q)/2J(Q) below by 4
and 6, respectively. With rational 2-torsion zero, the Mordell-Weil
ranks have the same lower bounds; the full rank is at least 10.

3. Geometric Néron-Severi upper bounds

Point counts over F_7 and F_49 are (14,56) for C0 and (12,60) for C1.
The degree-four Frobenius polynomials are respectively

  x^4+6x^3+21x^2+42x+49,      x^4+4x^3+13x^2+28x+49.

For a product of elliptic factors over Q at good reduction these
would factor into reciprocal quadratics x^2-t_1 x+7 and
x^2-t_2 x+7 with t_i integers. The discriminants for t_1,t_2 are
8 and 20, respectively, so neither factorization occurs. Thus both
surfaces are Q-simple. Their different Frobenius polynomials preclude
a Q-isogeny between them.

The geometric Néron-Severi group tensored with Q_l (l different
from 7) injects by the cycle-class map into H^2(1). Each geometric
divisor class is defined over a finite extension. It therefore
contributes only a root-of-unity eigenvalue for Frobenius on H^2(1).
The H^1 eigenvalues of J(C) are given by the two polynomials above;
on H^2 take their pairwise products. The checker calculates the
degree-28 characteristic polynomial by Newton sums, removes four
factors x-7, and certifies that the remaining 24 roots divided by 7
include no root of unity. It compares against all cyclotomic orders
m with phi(m)<=24 via exact polynomial gcd at an auxiliary prime
where the leading coefficients survive. The finite search covers
m<=2*24^2 by phi(m)>=sqrt(m/2).

An independent check uses the prime 20479. The two Frobenius
polynomials split with roots (623,8873,13278,18178) and
(3528,9830,10327,17269). Of the 28 pairwise products divided by
7 modulo 20479, four equal 1. The remaining multiplicative orders
are 3413, 6826, 10239, and 20478, all greater than 2*24^2. Reduction
cannot increase the order of a root of unity.

The four factors x-7 consist of the two complementary Frobenius
pairs from each surface. Thus each geometric Picard number is at
most 2; the product has geometric Picard number at most 4. The full
rational rank is >=10, while g+rho-1<=7, for the usual quadratic
Chabauty sufficient inequality rank<g+rho-1. For either simple
factor the right side is at most 3 and rank is at least 4.
Higher-depth methods, special p-adic dependencies, and the larger
fiber products with further columns are separate questions. This
calculation does not classify the (3,5) solutions or rule out a
five-row value.
