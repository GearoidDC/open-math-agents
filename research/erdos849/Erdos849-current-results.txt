Erdős 849 — results and verification

Every value a occurring in at least five rows of the left half of Pascal's
triangle satisfies a>10^105 and has at least sixteen distinct prime factors.
If a≥10^524, it has at least seventeen. A separate ordinary mathematical
argument gives at least 840 prime factors when a≥10^(10^115).
The existence of a single five-row value remains open.

The extension through 10^105

The finite base through 10^100 is described in Erdos849-reproduce.txt and
Erdos849-googol-note.txt. The nine fixed-pair classifications and the
published base through 10^60 are from Theorem 2.2 of Blokhuis, Brouwer
and de Weger (https://arxiv.org/pdf/1707.06893).

Five rows require four distinct proper columns. For a in (10^100,10^105],
at most one column in {2,3,4,6} can occur. Thus at least two proper columns
are ≥7. If the largest column is ≥10, it must collide with another column
≥7. A Python prime-residue sieve and exact inversions covered every such
pair. The largest possible column is 176. There were 14,362 column pairs,
351,330,164,203 initial row/column triples and 571,552 exact comparisons.
The search found zero collisions.

If the largest column is ≤9, the published classifications leave ten
possible four-column sets. A separate Node CRT sieve covered all ten,
spanning 87,703,536,006,922 row-pattern pairs. It tested 417,901,661 joined
residue candidates and found zero survivors. Its binomial row endpoints
were checked by independent division-free falling-product comparisons.
These tests cover every possible factorization of a.

To reproduce the added interval:

  node Erdos849GoogolLow.cjs
  python -u Erdos849GoogolHigh.py

The programs need only Node.js and standard Python. The Lean theorem
Erdos849Interval105.five_rows_above_cutoff combines the classified pairs,
the previous base and the two external numerical certificates.

Prime-factor restrictions

Erdos849-sixteen-primes.txt gives the sixteen-prime proof and the stronger
seventeen-prime result beyond 10^524. Erdos849-verification.txt gives the
rebuild and replay commands. The sixteen-prime proof includes independent
Python and Node searches over eighteen bounded four-column intervals.

Erdos849ReciprocalRunge.txt gives a different argument for high values.
For any fixed prime-count cap W, sufficiently large values with ≤W prime
factors must have at least as many prime factors as the product of all
their proper column indices. The height threshold can be computed from W.
The proof uses monodromy of the binomial polynomials and Pierre Dèbes,
"Hilbert subsets and s-integral points", Theorem 1.4 and its explicit
height estimate. Four distinct proper columns avoiding the published
forbidden pairs have product ≥840. Selecting two, three or four of
them according to their size gives the stated threshold 10^(10^115).
Erdos849RungeConstants.py checks the specialization using integer
arithmetic. This geometric argument is written in ordinary mathematics.
https://pro.univ-lille.fr/fileadmin/user_upload/pages_pros/pierre_debes/A18-HITBounds.pdf

Additional constructive tests

The sixth through eighth Lind–Singmaster values have exactly three rows.
The 3,120,256-digit eighth member was independently checked by a second
program from the archive. An explicit local argument and finite rational
tests also classify the entire infinite family within 10,000 rows of its
known consecutive pair. The only extra proper occurrence anywhere in that
band is C(78,2)=3003 for the first member. See Erdos849LindBand.md.

  python Erdos849RungeConstants.py
  python Erdos849LindBand.py --radius 10000
  node Erdos849LindIndependent.cjs 8

Lean checks the indicated deductions with the numerical claims passed as
explicit hypotheses. The residue searches run outside the Lean kernel.
None of these results rules out all candidates with arbitrarily many prime
factors or constructs all of the multiplicities asked for in Erdős 849.
