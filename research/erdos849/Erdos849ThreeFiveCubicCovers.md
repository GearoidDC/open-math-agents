# Cubic descent on the genus-two quotient of columns 3 and 5

Let $C_0$ be the smooth genus-two curve with equation

    v² = f(r) = 400r⁶ + 200r³ − 80r + 9.

For $\binom n3=\binom m5$, put $X=n-1$, $Y=m-2$, $r=X/Y$ and $v=2Y^2-5-20r^3$. The single point at infinity of the binomial curve maps to the rational point $q$ on $C_0$ at which $v/r^3$ approaches $+20$. Its hyperelliptic conjugate is distinct. The derivation is in [the genus-four curve note](Erdos849ThreeFiveCurve.txt).

**Proposition.** For every connected cyclic étale cover of degree three of $C_0$ over the algebraic closure of the rationals, the open curve obtained by deleting the three points above $q$ has only constant invertible regular functions. Thus these covers do not give the units required for Bilu’s criterion. The integral-point problem for columns 3 and 5 remains open.

## The forty covers form one Galois orbit

Corvaja, Lombardo and Zannier identify the covers with lines in the four-dimensional space $J(C_0)[3]$. Choosing a generator amounts to choosing its sign. There are 40 lines. At the good primes 7 and 11 the characteristic polynomials of Frobenius are respectively

    T⁴ + 6T³ + 21T² + 42T + 49,
    T⁴ + 5T³ + 18T² + 55T + 121.

The replay counts points over the prime field and its quadratic extension. The two polynomials are squarefree modulo 3, so their companion matrices give the corresponding linear actions in suitable bases. On the lines the first has ten cycles of length 4 and the second has four cycles of length 10. Any proper Galois orbit must therefore have size 20. In the companion basis for the second Frobenius it must be one of the six unions of two of its four cycles. [Corvaja, Lombardo and Zannier, Lemmas 2.6 and 2.8](https://arxiv.org/html/2411.17930v2#S2)

At $p=23470571$ the reduction of $f$ has exactly one double root $a$, the other four being simple. For the integer representative $a$, $f(a)$ is divisible by $p$ exactly once. The replay verifies these assertions by modular gcd, evaluation and the second derivative. Dividing by the square unit 400 makes the polynomial monic. Hensel factorization separates the double root and its complementary simple roots. Translating by $a$ makes the quadratic factor Eisenstein. Hence the local polynomial has type $1-\{2\}$, and inertia contains a transvection on the 3-torsion. [Anni and Dokchitser, Lemma 2.9](https://discovery.ucl.ac.uk/id/eprint/10089730/1/IGP-finalacceptedversion.pdf)

For each of the six sets the replay checks all rank-one unipotent transformations $v\mapsto v+uL(v)$, with $L(u)=0$, up to inversion. Scaling the coefficient by $-1$ gives the inverse, which stabilizes exactly the same subsets. None preserves any of the six sets. This excludes an orbit of size 20.

## Two local tests for the same torsion condition

For a decomposition $f=P^2-λH^3$, scale the square coordinate and $P$ by $λ$. The resulting square-minus-cube decomposition is $\lambda^2 f=(\lambda P)^2-(\lambda H)^3$. The associated cubic cover has a genus-one quotient $E$ with plane equation

    w³ − 3λ H(r) w − 2λ P(r) = 0.

The three points above $q$ have as their images the three points of $E$ on the line at infinity. The three choices of quotient involution are conjugate by deck transformations and permute this set. In particular, the exponent of the subgroup generated by all pair differences of the three images, if finite, is independent of that choice. It is also preserved by Galois because $q$ is rational. Transitivity on covers makes this finite exponent common to all forty if it exists. [Corvaja, Lombardo and Zannier, Proposition 2.13 and Lemma 2.17](https://arxiv.org/html/2411.17930v2#S2)

The following coefficients are in ascending order.

| Prime | $λ$ | $H$ | $P$ | $\#E(\mathbb F_p)$ | Order of a pair difference over $\mathbb F_{p^3}$ |
|---|---:|---|---|---:|---:|
| 13 | 4 | (8,2,1) | (9,0,12,1) | 15 | 12 |
| 61 | 24 | (43,5,1) | (55,6,27,27) | 68 | 665 |

Both rows satisfy the decomposition coefficient by coefficient. Each lifts over the corresponding $p$-adic integers by multivariate Hensel. Indeed the seven variables are the four coefficients of $P$, the two lower coefficients of monic $H$, and $λ$. A vector in the kernel of the derivative satisfies $2P\,\delta P=H^2(H\,\delta\lambda+3\lambda\,\delta H)$. Since $f$ is squarefree modulo $p$, $H$ and $P$ are coprime. Then $H^2$ divides $δP$, whose degree is at most three. Thus $δP=0$, and the degree bound on $δH$ gives $δλ=δH=0$. The decomposition scheme has finitely many geometric characteristic-zero points by the 3-torsion description, so these lifts correspond to algebraic covers.

The discriminant in $w$ of the affine cubic is $-108\lambda^2 f(r)$, whose roots are simple. A singular affine point would force a repeated discriminant root. At infinity the cubic has three distinct conjugate points in both reductions, since $w^3-3λH_2w-2λP_3$ is irreducible over each prime field. Thus $E$ has good reduction. The local Frobenius cyclically permutes its three infinity points, so all three pair differences have the same order. The replay performs the chord-and-tangent law on the projective cubic over the explicit extension of degree three, using one of the infinity points as origin. It starts with the group order over that extension and removes each unnecessary prime factor.

Good reduction preserves the 2-primary part of the order of every torsion point at both odd primes. If the boundary images generated a finite subgroup in characteristic zero, its exponent would therefore have to be both even and odd by the table. Consequently some boundary difference has infinite order. Its preimage difference on the cover also has infinite order.

Finally the degree-zero divisors supported on the three boundary points form an augmentation lattice of rank two. After tensoring with the rationals the cyclic deck group acts irreducibly, since its generator has minimal polynomial $T^2+T+1$. The map to the Jacobian is equivariant and has nonzero image after tensoring, hence it is injective there. No nonzero integral divisor on that boundary is principal. A unit on the open cover must therefore be constant.
